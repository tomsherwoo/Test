## 1. European fund distribution platforms and networks: what they sell fund houses (Allfunds, MFEX/Euroclear, Clearstream Fund Centre, Inversis, German B2B platforms)

### Takeaway
The pan-European fund-distribution utilities make money from fund houses mainly in two ways. The first is distribution-intelligence data: Allfunds Telemetrics 2.0 and its Market Intelligence module, and Euroclear FundsPlace Market Intelligence. The second is content channels into distributor portals: Allfunds "Fund Insight" and Clearstream Fund Centre "Compass". I found no evidence of banner-ad packages and no published prices. The utilities are consolidating into two groups:
- **Deutsche Börse:** Clearstream Fund Centre plus Allfunds. The Allfunds deal was approved by shareholders in March 2026 and is expected to complete in H1 2027.
- **Euroclear:** FundsPlace plus MFEX, fully integrated on 1 January 2026, plus a reported stake in Inversis in Spain.

The German B2B depot banks (FNZ Bank/ebase/Fondsdepot Bank, FFB, MorgenFund) look like operational platforms rather than media owners. Visible fund-house marketing in that channel happens at broker-pool (Maklerpool) and network events instead, for example Fonds Finanz's MMM: about 150 exhibitors and many IDD-certified talks.

### Inferences
- **Best "point-of-research" spend for a UK boutique.** The assumed advertiser distributes Irish and Luxembourg UCITS. On the continental utilities, its most valuable spend is probably distributor-flow data (Telemetrics 2.0 or Euroclear Market Intelligence). That data can show which distributors already buy peer funds in its category, so sales effort can be targeted. Next would be content placed inside distributor portals (Allfunds Fund Insight, Clearstream Compass), rather than advertising.
- **Flows, not page views.** The products are described in terms of flows and AuA by distributor. I found nothing showing that they report "which distributor viewed your fund page".
- **Platform listing comes first.** Being onboarded to Allfunds or Euroclear FundsPlace is what makes a fund findable by continental bank and wealth selectors. "Digital Onboarding" cuts the set-up time, which matters for a boutique without local sales teams.
- **Consolidation.** Once the Deutsche Börse–Allfunds deal completes (expected H1 2027), one group will own both Allfunds and Clearstream Fund Centre. Euroclear already combines FundsPlace and MFEX and, reportedly, Inversis. Expect the fund-house data and visibility products to be repackaged or repriced in 2027. Time contract renewals with that in mind.
- **German channel.** The German depot banks look like service providers, not media owners. A fund house reaches German brokers and advisers through pool and network events and partner meetings (Fonds Finanz MMM, CARAT), where IDD-certified sessions are a draw. That fits IDD's 15-hour annual training requirement for insurance intermediaries, which I did not re-check in this session.
- **Inducement risk.** Buying data from a platform carries lower inducement risk than paying distributors. Paying a platform for distributor-facing "visibility" would still need checking against MiFID II inducement rules in each distributor's country. Another researcher covers this.

## 2. UK adviser platforms and wraps: marketing packages, events, flow data, and best-buy lists

### Takeaway
UK platform payment rules (PS13/1, now in COBS 6.1E) bar platforms from being paid by fund managers, with narrow exceptions. The exceptions are reported to include payments for advertising products on the platform and for management information. So any platform "marketing package" has to be a reasonable fee for a real service that does not buy preferential treatment. Within the budget I verified 2026 status only for aberdeen's Wrap, which aberdeen still owns. I found no published fund-manager marketing packages for any UK adviser platform.

### Cited Findings
- **What PS13/1 set out (2013):**
  - It set the rules on payments from product providers to platforms, to limit providers' influence over distribution.
  - Platforms are to be paid through platform charges agreed with the client.
  - Payments "from alternative sources in the distribution chain" do not count as "platform charges".
  - Sources: [FCA PS13/1 (PDF)](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps13-1.pdf); [FCA PS13/1 page](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/ps13-1-payments-platform-service-providers-and-cash-rebates-providers)
- **Permitted provider payments, as reported:**
  - Payments "for advertising products on the platform".
  - Payments for work providing the product provider with management information about consumers invested in the product.
  - Such payments must not vary inappropriately, and are allowed for "legitimate expenses".
  - Sources: [Pinsent Masons Out-Law guide](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/guides/-platforms-and-remuneration); [Out-Law news](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/news/fca-platform-market-payment-rules-and-cash-rebating-ban-outlined). This is from search summaries only; I could not open the current COBS 6.1E text in the FCA Handbook.
- **Cash rebates:** banned on non-advised platforms to stop them disguising platform charges, with a de minimis exception of £1 per month per fund — [FCA PS13/1](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/policy/ps13-1.pdf)
- **Adviser-side rule:** COBS 6.1A.14A is reported to stop advisers recommending where product or platform charges would offset adviser or platform charges, or where a banned cash rebate would arise. It refers to COBS 6.1E.10R(2) as a permitted exception. Not checked against the current Handbook — [FCA 2013/43 instrument](https://www.Handbook.fca.org.uk/instrument/2013/FCA_2013_43.pdf)
- **Market debate:** whether platforms should take provider marketing payments has been argued in trade press ("Are provider-to-platform marketing payments acceptable?") — [Professional Adviser (fact source only)](https://professionaladviser.com/feature/2280595/-providertoplatform-marketing-payments-acceptable)
- **aberdeen Wrap, 2026 status:**
  - aberdeen still owns its adviser platform. Its Q3 2026 trading update reported Adviser AUMA of £84.5bn, up 5% from £80.4bn at 31 December 2025, with Q3 net outflows of £0.7bn — [DirectorsTalk summary of aberdeen's Q3 2026 update](https://www.directorstalkinterviews.com/aberdeen-group-reports-578-9bn-auma-and-strong-interactive-investor-inflows/4121267238)
  - In August 2025 aberdeen agreed to sell Aberdeen Financial Planning to Ascot Lloyd, and will keep working with Ascot Lloyd through the Wrap platform — [aberdeen plc](https://www.aberdeenplc.com/en-gb/news/all-news/aberdeen-agrees-sale-of-financial-planning-business); [Private Banker International](https://www.privatebankerinternational.com/news/aberdeen-divest-financial-planning-ascot-lloyd/)
  - aberdeen took adviser service in-house from FNZ. FNZ still provides Wrap's operating platform, custody and settlement — [Financial Planning Today (fact source)](https://financialplanningtoday.co.uk/news/aberdeen-shifts-wrap-client-service-team-from-fnz)
  - In 2021-era reporting, Wrap and Elevate "operate on the same core technology", and the then Standard Life Aberdeen was exploring a sale of Parmenion — [Private Banker International](https://www.privatebankerinternational.com/news/standard-life-aberdeen-to-divest-financial-advisory-business-parmenion/)

### Inferences
- **Legitimate levers on UK adviser platforms (to confirm platform by platform):**
  1. Paid advertising, where a platform offers it, priced as a reasonable fee.
  2. Paying for management information on holdings and flows of your funds.
  3. Inclusion on merit in platform research, fund ranges or model portfolio service (MPS) ranges.
  4. Speaking at platform-run adviser events.
- **Best-buy lists.** "Selected fund" or best-buy lists are mostly a direct-to-consumer feature, not an adviser-platform one. Paying for placement on any such list is the "preferential treatment" the platform payment rules were meant to stop. This is my inference; the compliance researcher should confirm it.
- **Data products are the defensible spend.** For a boutique, the clearest options are platform management information on its own funds and making sure its data is complete in the tools advisers use to choose funds (see question 3).

This applies to Transact, Quilter, Aviva, Fidelity Adviser Solutions, AJ Bell Investcentre, Nucleus, Parmenion, 7IM, M&G Wealth Platform, Seccl, Fundment, Hubwise and Wealthtime.

## 3. Research, ratings and analytics tools used for fund screening: paid placements, provider hubs, data on adviser usage

### Takeaway
I could verify two levers that put a fund manager inside the adviser's working tools:
- **FE fundinfo's FE Analytics**, used by more than 3,900 UK adviser firms according to current FE material. Its manager-facing market-intelligence functions show how advisers sort and see a manager's funds, and which funds are most bought and sold in a sector. FE also runs an MPS directory with more than 75 providers.
- **Adviser risk-profiling tools, chiefly Dynamic Planner.** Over 150 asset managers partner with it to have funds or model portfolio services risk-profiled (more than 1,700 profiled funds), so they show up in the adviser's suitability workflow.

Fintel (Defaqto plus SimplyBiz; it bought RSMR in January 2025) openly sells "data, distribution, and marketing and promotion services to product providers". None of these firms publishes prices, and models where the fund manager pays the rater create a pay-to-play perception risk.

### Inferences
- **Risk-tool presence is the most literal "point of work".** For UK IFAs, being risk-profiled in the adviser's risk tool (Dynamic Planner, and probably others; see Gaps) puts a fund inside the suitability workflow, because advisers filter by risk profile. For a boutique this means paying to profile only the funds that fit advised portfolios (multi-asset, income, global equity) and checking they appear in adviser filters. The Schroders co-branded range shows the far end of this approach. A boutique would more realistically aim for a few profiled funds or one risk-mapped MPS.
- **FE Analytics is the closest verified UK answer to "who looked at my fund".** It shows how advisers sort and see a manager's funds, and what is bought and sold in its sectors. Named-adviser view data is not confirmed. Under GDPR, firm-level or aggregate data seems more likely.
- **Concentration in Fintel.** Fintel now holds Defaqto ratings, RSMR ratings, SimplyBiz's adviser membership and "marketing and promotion services" sold to providers. That is commercially useful but raises the pay-to-play perception (paid promotion and ratings under one roof). Flag it for compliance.
- **Data quality is marketing.** Screening tools are where funds get found and dropped, so complete and accurate data in FE fundinfo and Morningstar costs little and helps visibility. This is my inference, not a sourced finding.
