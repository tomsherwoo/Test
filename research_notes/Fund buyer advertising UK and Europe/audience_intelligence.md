## 1. UK segment map, sizes and concentration of decision-making

### Takeaway
The UK intermediary market is a long tail of about 5,500 advice firms and about 31,000 advisers (88% of firms are "small"). Decision power over fund flows is concentrating fast in a much smaller set of central investment teams:
- about 500 discretionary/wealth firms, where the top 10 hold about 59% of discretionary AUM;
- a £208–214bn MPS market growing 24–32% a year and now equal to 21–27% of adviser-platform assets;
- roughly 25–40 PE- or provider-backed consolidators, 84% of which run in-house MPS;
- St. James's Place's single centrally governed network of about 4,950 advisers.

A few hundred buying centres plausibly control the majority of intermediated fund flows.

### Inferences
- **How concentrated is UK decision-making?** Combining the findings above gives a target universe of very roughly 100–300 central buying units. That set likely influences the majority of UK intermediated fund flows:
  - the ~500 FCA-counted investment managers, where the top 10 run ~59% of discretionary AUM;
  - MPS at 21–27% of platform assets, growing 24–32% a year, with Tatton dominant in Defaqto recommendation data;
  - ~25–40 consolidators, 84% of which run their own MPS;
  - SJP's ~4,950 advisers under one investment proposition;
  - plus platform fund-list teams and ratings/research houses.

  This is an estimate from the cited structure, not a published figure.
- **Share of advisers outsourcing.** No 2025–26 headcount figure was found. Asset proxies suggest about a fifth to a quarter of adviser-platform assets sit in MPS (21% NextWealth; 27% Platforum). Including DFM models the share approaches two-fifths on at least one major platform (Quilter, 41.9%). The direction is up: a net 21% of advisers plan to raise MPS allocations.
- **Targeting angle: MPS teams.** MPS teams are the UK's highest-leverage audience. They are few, named and senior, with rebalancing cadences, and a single inclusion can feed hundreds of adviser firms. The long tail of ~5,500 mostly small adviser firms mainly decides which MPS, DFM and platform to use, not which single fund to buy. Messaging to the tail is therefore about being in the models and on the platforms they already use.
- **Targeting angle: paraplanners.** Paraplanners are the hidden research gatekeepers in medium and large firms (77–89% employ them). In small firms (31% employ one; 36% use none), the adviser-owner is the researcher. Persona targeting should split on firm size.
- **Targeting angle: consolidators.** Consolidator in-house MPS and fund ranges (84% and 68%) can lock third-party funds out of hundreds of acquired firms at once. Consolidator CIOs and investment committees are therefore make-or-break accounts. After an acquisition, target firms usually migrate to the acquirer's CIP, so acquired firms stop being independent fund buyers.

## 2. Continental Europe segment map and sizes by country

### Takeaway
Continental Europe has very large registered intermediary populations, but far fewer real fund selectors:
- Italy: 54,783 consulenti finanziari, about 24,500 of them in the main Assoreti networks.
- Germany: 41,388 §34f intermediaries, but only about 375 fee-based §34h advisers and 358 VuV member firms.
- France: about 7,000 CIFs, with insurance generating 67% of CGP revenue.
- Switzerland: about 1,300–1,350 FINMA-licensed independent asset managers (about 1,567 portfolio managers and trustees still supervised), 56% with three people or fewer.
- Spain: only 88 EAFs plus 52 EAFN, advising just €17bn.

Selection power sits mostly in centralised bank, network, insurer and platform teams. Independent channels are fragmented long tails.

- **Italy: dual targeting.** Italy has a large, reachable individual-adviser base: about 54.8k registered, about 38k operating and about 24.5k in the big networks.   2. tens of thousands of consulenti, who decide allocation within the shelf.

  OCF's 2026 messaging about "finfluencers" signals regulatory sensitivity to social-media promotion in Italy.
- **France: insurers and platforms decide.** Insurance is 67% of CGP revenue (CNCGP), so the decisive gatekeepers for a UCITS fund are likely the life insurers' and platforms' unités de compte fund lists, not each CGP individually. CGPs then choose within those lists. Associations (CNCGP: 2,521 cabinets; ANACOFI: 3,020 CIFs) define the reachable long tail.
- **Spain: banks dominate.** The independent advisory channel is tiny (140 EAF/EAFN firms, €17bn advised). For a foreign UCITS manager, Spanish demand is mostly decided by bank and bank-owned gestora selectors and fund-of-funds/discretionary teams. The EAF/EAFN universe (about 140 firms) is small enough to treat entirely as named accounts.
- **Germany: few genuine selectors.** The headline 41k §34f intermediaries are mostly product intermediaries; only about 375 are fee-based. The genuinely selector-like independent population is small: 358 VuV members and about 680 Wertpapierinstitute in total. Big captive banking groups and pools/platforms matter more. The 41k figure overstates the professional fund-selector audience by more than an order of magnitude.
- **Switzerland: a precise long tail.** About 1,300–1,350 licensed independent asset managers (about 1,567 including trustees), mostly tiny (56% with three people or fewer), form a long tail that a public FINMA list can enumerate. That makes Switzerland one of Europe's most precisely targetable independent segments. Founder retirement and consolidation (ValIndex) gives an angle on next-generation and succession themes.
- **Pan-European selector population.** No census exists. Survey programmes reach about 800–900 selectors a year across 8–10 countries. The core professional selector population outside individual advisers (bank, private bank, insurer, fund-of-funds and platform selectors) is plausibly in the low thousands. This is an inference.

## 3. Decision-making units and the buying journey

### Takeaway
UK and European fund buying is a committee process. Research and selection teams screen on data platforms, so discovery overwhelmingly happens on research and data platforms: 78.3% of UK selectors versus 4.7% via direct manager contact. Due diligence and investment-committee approval follow before a fund goes onto buy lists, models or shelves. Consumer Duty, MPS concentration and consolidators' in-house ranges are narrowing the number of doors. Trust in team stability and clear, jargon-free communication helps get through them.

### Cited Findings
- **Discovery.** In FE fundinfo's survey of 978 UK fund selectors (published about mid-2025), 78.3% said they most often discover new funds through fund research platforms such as Trustnet. Only 4.7% said direct contact from fund managers. This is FE fundinfo's own marketing material and favours its platform — [FE fundinfo](https://www.fefundinfo.com/insights/where-uk-fund-selectors-really-find-new-ideas); [FE fundinfo Trustnet whitepaper](https://www.fefundinfo.com/landing-pages/inst/trustnet-whitepaper)
- **What selectors value.** Broadridge Fund Brand 50 2026 (US) found selectors weigh "solidity and client-oriented thinking" highly. Gatekeepers also prioritise **investment team stability as a proxy for repeatable performance** — [Broadridge FB50 2026 via Finviz](https://finviz.com/news/341051/blackrock-retains-top-spot-in-the-us-2026-broadridge-fund-brand-50-report). In Europe, Broadridge reports selectors want "targeted communication that is easy to understand and in more engaging formats" — [Institutional Asset Manager](https://institutionalassetmanager.co.uk/the-best-performing-fund-brands-in-europe-and-globally-broadridge/)
- **What Broadridge measures.** Fund Buyer Focus covers selectors' decision processes on brand, sales, marketing, manager selection and asset allocation, which shows these as distinct stages and influences — [Broadridge](https://www.broadridge.com/financial-services/asset-management/mutual-funds-etf/access-unique-distribution-insights/fbf-intelligence)
- **Research friction (UK advisers).** In a survey for The Investment Association (261 advisers, fieldwork December 2024), advisers' main frustrations when researching funds were:
  - jargon in fund documents;
  - vague descriptions of what a fund aims to do;
  - difficulty finding detail on how the fund is run.

  Source: [The Investment Association, SDR adviser research, February 2025](https://www.theia.org/sites/default/files/2025-02/SDR%20investor%20and%20adviser%20awareness,%20understanding%20and%20expectations%202025_0.pdf)
- **Fewer, larger relationships under Consumer Duty.** Advisers partnered with 2.5 DFMs on average in 2020 but only 1.3 in 2024, "due to growing complexity and a need for consistency under Consumer Duty", before rising to 1.7 in 2025 — [IFA Magazine](https://ifamagazine.com/discretionary-mps-assets-set-to-surpass-200bn-after-surging-a-third-in-a-year/). Advisers want an industry standard for MPS due diligence — [IFA Magazine](https://ifamagazine.com/advisers-crave-industry-wide-standard-for-mps-due-diligence/)
- **Gatekeeper lock-in.** 84% of profiled consolidators run in-house MPS and 68% have their own fund range (NextWealth 2025, via commentary) — [Evidence Investor](https://www.evidenceinvestor.com/post/financial-advice-consolidation-uk). Tatton propositions took 62% of top-10 MPS recommendation value in Q2 2025 — [Professional Paraplanner (Defaqto data)](https://professionalparaplanner.co.uk/defaqto-reveals-top-10-most-recommended-mps/)
- **Researcher role by firm size.** 77–89% of medium and large adviser firms employ paraplanners directly, against 31% of small firms — [Professional Paraplanner (FCA survey)](https://professionalparaplanner.co.uk/development/what-the-fcas-2025-advice-market-survey-means-for-you/)
- **Regulatory scrutiny.** The FCA flagged a closer look at MPS (February 2025) and is progressing an MPS review (2026) — [Professional Paraplanner](https://professionalparaplanner.co.uk/defaqto-reveals-top-10-most-recommended-mps/); [Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/united-kingdom/wealth-management/fca-wealth-management-survey-2026-key-insights-and-fca-expectations-for-wealth-managers)
- **Consideration triggers and demand shifts:**
  - about 70% of pan-European selectors are now familiar with ELTIFs, up from about 50% in 2024 (Research in Finance/Neuberger Berman) — [Funds Europe](https://funds-europe.com/fund-selectors-turn-to-private-markets-in-2025-research-shows/);
  - 36% of UK advisers increased exposure to alternatives and 32% to emerging markets over 12 months (Schroders, 221 advisers, 2025) — [IFA Magazine](https://ifamagazine.com/schroders-uk-financial-adviser-survey-2025-advisers-look-to-diversify-portfolios-to-strengthen-resilience/);
  - only 17% of European fund selectors and intermediaries say active ETFs are among the ETF types they use most, but 24% plan to use them more (Cerulli; attribution via search extract) — [Cerulli](https://www.cerulli.com/press-releases/passive-investment-is-thriving-amid-market-turbulence-in-europe).
- **How many are in market.** The LinkedIn B2B Institute and Ehrenberg-Bass "95:5 rule" holds that about 5% of B2B buyers are in market at any time. It derives from purchase cycles: a provider switched about every five years means about 20% of buyers in market a year and about 5% a quarter. It is a heuristic and varies by category — [Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/peter-weinberg-jon-lombardo-95-5-rule)
- **Communication counts in selection.** Research in Finance's European Fund Selector Study ranks "top asset managers for communication and events" as one of its topics — [Research in Finance](https://researchinfinance.co.uk/our-studies/european-fund-selector-study/)

### Inferences
- **Typical journey, synthesised from the above:**
  1. Trigger: a portfolio gap or theme demand (alternatives, EM, private markets), a replacement after a manager or team change, or a cost/value review under Consumer Duty.
  2. Screening on research and data platforms (discovery).
  3. Shortlist and quantitative due diligence.
  4. Qualitative due diligence and manager meeting.
  5. Investment-committee approval.
  6. Buy-list, model or shelf inclusion.
  7. Ongoing monitoring, quarterly model rebalancing and periodic reviews.

  Manager outreach influences mainly steps 3–5. Data quality and presence on platforms decide step 2.
- **What likely kills consideration:**
  - investment-team instability (the FB50 emphasis);
  - opaque or jargon-heavy documentation (IA survey);
  - not being on the buyer's platforms or in their models (concentration and in-house ranges);
  - poor fit with value and fair-value evidence under Consumer Duty.
- **How the units differ:**
  - **UK advisers** mostly choose MPS, DFMs and platforms, plus some direct fund picks in CIPs, and paraplanners do the research in larger firms.
  - **UK DFM/MPS teams** select funds directly, through analysts and investment committees, often with quarterly model rebalances.
  - **European bank selectors** curate shelves and fund-of-funds for captive networks, and individual advisers allocate within them.
- **Creative angle: always-on presence.** With about 5% in market, always-on presence in the tools and spaces selectors already use beats bursts. Look for timing signals: model rebalancing windows, and manager departures at competing funds, which trigger replacement searches.

### Gaps
- No 2024–26 data was retrieved on:
  - investment-committee calendars or cadence (monthly versus quarterly);
  - the number of manager meetings selectors take;
  - minimum track-record or AUM thresholds;
  - the share of selectors using FE Analytics, Morningstar Direct or similar tools;
  - quantified ratings-house influence (Square Mile, RSMR, FE Investments, Morningstar, Defaqto).

  The search budget ran out before these targeted searches.
- European bank-selector process specifics (country differences, the role of central ManCos) are not sourced here.

### Inferences
- **Targeting angle: be where screening happens.** Selectors discover funds in research and data platforms, not through outreach. So the "media" with most reach at the moment of consideration is the fund's own data footprint:
  - complete, accurate and timely data on the platforms selectors screen (FE fundinfo/FE Analytics, Morningstar);
  - fund pages and factsheets that convert platform referrals.

  Retargeting visitors to fund pages and factsheets, and IP- or account-based targeting of known buying centres, follows naturally.
- **Targeting angle: AI answer engines.** With 41% of UK advisers actively using AI and agentic tools coming, answer engines are becoming a research layer in their own right. Making fund information machine-readable, consistent and quotable (plain-English objectives, team, process, risks) may matter as much as paid media. The IA's jargon complaint makes clarity both a creative and a technical requirement.
- **Format.** Video calls (Italy 77%; UK 69% among wealth managers) and email dominate. Short, clear, PM-led video and plain-English explainers fit both the format and the "easy to understand, engaging" ask.
- **Germany.** Xing is no longer a viable B2B content network for fund selectors (it is a recruiting product). LinkedIn is the professional network of record in DACH; Xing is at most for employer brand or recruiting.

## 5. Data sources to enumerate target accounts and people (and UK/EU data-protection implications)

### Takeaway
UK targets can be enumerated more precisely than almost anywhere in Europe:
- the FCA Register has a free single-lookup API plus a paid bulk Register Extract Service (RES);
- ISS Market Intelligence, after buying Autus (70k+ firms and 220k individuals from the FCA Register and Directory), maps flows and MPS usage to named adviser firms.

Continental registers (FINMA, CNMV, DIHK, ORIAS, OCF) give firm and person lists of varying depth. The main legal constraints:
- **UK:** emailing corporate subscribers needs no PECR consent but does need a documented legitimate-interests assessment. Sole traders and non-LLP partnerships need consent or the soft opt-in.
- **EU:** Germany is strict (consent for B2B email); France and the Netherlands are more permissive.

### Cited Findings

**UK registers**
- **FCA Register API.** It is designed for individual look-ups, not bulk access: one entity per query, with rate limits. Bulk data comes from the paid Register Extract Service (RES): weekly or monthly subscriptions or one-off extracts, delivered by Spectrum Data Management for the FCA (RES Subscribers' Handbook, April 2026) — [FCA RES handbook](https://www.fca.org.uk/publication/documents/register-extract-handbook.pdf)
- The FCA says the Register and extracts "can also be purchased for a fee or accessed through a free application programming interface (API)" — [FCA privacy notice](https://www.fca.org.uk/privacy/personal-data-financial-services-register-services)
- A secondary source says RES is a file-based bulk download, not an API — [Hackford Data](https://hackforddata.com/insights/fca-register-extract-service-res). A reseller cites about £20k a year, with enquiries to data.extract@fca.org.uk; this is unverified, so check with the FCA — [TOVO Data](https://www.tovodata.co.uk/research/where-can-i-access-the-full-fca-register)
- Community tooling exists: a Python wrapper documents Register API usage — [readthedocs](https://financial-services-register-api.readthedocs.io/en/latest/sources/usage.html). Third-party scrapers also exist — [Apify](https://apify.com/danielainsworth/fca-register). Scraping carries terms-of-use risk, and the FCA Register API's own terms could not be read.
- The most-cited FCA API terms page in search results covers the FCA *Handbook* API, not the Register API. It restricts use, requires data security and asserts FCA ownership of the data — [FCA Handbook T&Cs](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/footer-widget/terms-and-conditions)

**UK commercial intermediary data**
- **ISS Market Intelligence MarketPro** — [ISS MI](https://www.issmarketintelligence.com/?p=4815); [ISS MI MarketPro new features](https://insights.issgovernance.com/posts/iss-market-intelligence-adds-new-features-to-marketpro):
  - claims coverage of more than 90% of the UK retail market, using data from platforms, fund managers, and life and pension companies;
  - collects contact, flow and holdings data monthly, mapped to the individual firm;
  - views at firm, network, group and platform level, including model portfolios and off-platform assets, with three-year AUM history and regional splits;
  - a May 2025 update added model-provider flow tracking, showing **which adviser firms use each MPS provider**.

**UK GDPR / PECR**
- **Subscriber types (ICO).**
  - Corporate subscribers are companies, LLPs and Scottish partnerships. Sole traders and other partnerships are "individual subscribers", which PECR treats like individuals.
  - Email to corporate subscribers needs no PECR consent. Email to individual subscribers needs consent or the soft opt-in (existing customers only), so a bought list cannot be used to email sole traders.

  Sources: [ICO B2B marketing](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/); [ICO lawful basis](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/sending-direct-marketing-choosing-your-lawful-basis/)
- **UK GDPR still applies to named individuals' work emails**, usually under legitimate interests with a documented legitimate-interests assessment — [Harper James](https://harperjames.co.uk/article/b2b-marketing-and-gdpr-the-basics/); [Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/b2b-email-marketing-rules-uk/)
- **Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (secondary sources; verify commencement):**
  - adds wording to UK GDPR confirming direct marketing *may* be a legitimate interest; a balancing test is still needed;
  - raises the maximum PECR fine from £500k to £17.5m or 4% of global turnover, with commencement reported as staged (one source says February 2026);
  - adds a charitable soft opt-in.

  Source: [Global Law Experts](https://globallawexperts.com/b2b-email-marketing-rules-uk/)

**EU national rules on B2B email (vendor and legal blogs; verify against statute)**
- Germany: UWG §7 generally requires prior consent even for B2B email; the existing-customer exception under §7(3) has four conditions; enforcement is complaint-driven.
- France: the CNIL accepts legitimate interest for B2B email to professional addresses if the message relates to the recipient's professional role, with opt-out.
- Italy and Spain: contested. Italy's Codice Privacy/Garante is read as consent-based by some sources and legitimate-interest-based by others; for Spain, LSSI art. 21 is read as requiring consent.
- Netherlands: broadly permissive with opt-out.

Sources: [The Stacked Creator](https://thestackedcreator.com/articles/cold-email-gdpr/); [B2B Data Index](https://b2bdataindex.com/compliance/); [Quarvio](https://www.quarvio.io/blog/cold-email-gdpr-guide)

### Inferences
- **UK build-out.** A defensible UK target list can be built from several layers:
  1. FCA RES or Register API data, for firm legal form and permissions;
  2. FCA Directory data, for certified individuals;
  3. ISS MI MarketPro, for flows, platform use and MPS-provider usage per firm, which lets you prioritise firms by investment leverage;
  4. DFM/MPS and consolidator investment-team lists (manual research).

  Legal form drives what is allowed. If the 77% limited-company and 10% sole-trader split holds, most firms are corporate subscribers. Sole-trader and non-LLP partnership IFAs need consent for email, but can still be reached with paid social or programmatic matched to the firm or account, subject to the platforms' own rules and a legitimate-interests assessment.
- **Targeting angle: MPS usage as a list filter.** ISS MI's model-provider usage data allows a high-precision "MPS-by-adviser" map. Advisers using MPS providers that already hold the fund can be targeted with reinforcement. Advisers using providers that don't can be targeted to create pull-through demand on those providers.
- **Continental enumeration.** Switzerland and Spain are small enough for complete named-account coverage (about 1,300–1,350 licensed independent asset managers and about 140 EAF/EAFN). France and Italy need register-plus-association data because the populations are large. In Germany the §34f register is a weak proxy for fund selectors; VuV membership and BaFin Wertpapierinstitute are better.
- **Email by country.** Germany should default to consent-based email, with paid social and programmatic doing the cold reach. France and the Netherlands allow role-relevant B2B email under legitimate interest with opt-out.
- **Boundary cases under the no-trade-publications rule:**
  - **Trustnet** is owned by FE fundinfo and combines fund data with an editorial newsroom. Fund *data* presence is fine; advertising on Trustnet's editorial pages is a boundary case.
  - **Morningstar** (data plus editorial adviser sites): same logic.
  - **MandateWire** is a data product of FT Specialist, sibling to FT Adviser. It is a data source, not media, but owned by a trade-media group, so flag it.
  - **The Wealth Mosaic** (vendor directory with content) and **Wealth Summit** (Swiss events/studies publisher) are borderline content and event properties.
  - **Defaqto**, **Research in Finance**, **Dynamic Planner**, **NextWealth**, **Platforum** and **ISS MI** are research, data and software firms, not trade media.

### Gaps

## 6. Country nuances relevant to digital targeting

### Takeaway
Distribution structure varies more by country than media habits do:
- **Germany:** LinkedIn over Xing; strict consent-based email; a small independent-selector population.
- **Italy:** large network sales forces reachable individually, but shelves are decided centrally; video calls are the norm; regulators are wary of finfluencers.
- **France:** CGP demand runs through insurance wrappers, so insurer and platform fund lists come first.
- **Switzerland:** about 1,300–1,350 mostly tiny licensed independent asset managers that a public list can enumerate.
- **Spain:** bank selectors dominate; independents are tiny.

### Inferences
- **Localisation.** Germany, Italy, Spain and France have large domestic adviser populations who mostly work in their own language. Central selectors in Dublin, Luxembourg and Zurich, and pan-European private banks, are more likely to accept English. Localise long-tail creative and keep English for central selector accounts. This is an inference; no language-preference survey was found.
- **Italy.** The finfluencer sensitivity and the network-controlled shelf argue for running social activity through or with network relationships, and against broad influencer tactics.
- **Switzerland.** Many tiny firms run by founders nearing retirement makes "next-generation" content (succession, outsourced research, model portfolios for small teams) a plausible creative angle.

### Gaps
- No 2024–26 data was found on WhatsApp or Telegram use between asset managers and Italian or Spanish advisers. WhatsApp appears only as a lower-ranked client-contact channel in the ING/Finer survey.
- No source was found on cultural norms around vendor contact (for example, appetite for cold outreach in DACH against Southern Europe), language preference statistics, or LinkedIn penetration among fund selectors by country.
- Nordic, Benelux, Irish and Luxembourg nuances were not researched because the search budget ran out.
