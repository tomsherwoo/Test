## 1. Adoption: how far UK and European fund buyers use generative AI for fund research, due diligence and comparison

### Takeaway
Use of AI tools by UK advisers went from a minority to a large majority in 2025–26. Intelliflo found 74% in 2026, up from 43% a year earlier; FE fundinfo found 95% "have deployed AI tooling". Most of this use is admin work (meeting notes, documentation), not fund selection. The bigger change for fund discovery in 2026 is that the research tools selectors already pay for now have agents built in and pipe their data into ChatGPT, Claude and Microsoft 365 Copilot: Morningstar Direct AI, FE fundinfo Nexus, and connectors from LSEG, FactSet, S&P Global and Morningstar. I found no 2024–26 survey that measures generative-AI use by professional fund selectors specifically, such as UK DFM/MPS teams or continental bank selectors. That is a real evidence gap.

### Inferences
- Professional fund buyers in regulated firms most likely meet generative AI inside licensed or enterprise tools first: Morningstar Direct AI's manager-research agent, FE Nexus, M365 Copilot with Morningstar/LSEG/FactSet connectors, and ChatGPT or Claude for Financial Services. Consumer chatbots come second. So the completeness and accuracy of an asset manager's records at Morningstar, FE fundinfo, LSEG/Lipper, FactSet and S&P is now an AI-visibility issue, not just a data-operations chore.
- UK IFA AI use leans towards admin, so IFAs mostly meet funds through AI in platform and research tools, and through paraplanners' AI-assisted research write-ups. For DFM/MPS teams, multi-managers and bank selectors, Morningstar Direct's manager-research agent (October 2026) is probably the first agentic touchpoint at scale.
- Headline adoption figures use very broad definitions ("deployed AI tooling" includes meeting transcription). The report should not read them as "advisers use ChatGPT to pick funds".

## 2. Generative engine optimisation (GEO/AEO) for funds: how assistants source fund information and what makes it discoverable and accurate

### Takeaway
AI assistants build fund answers from three places. The first is search indexes: Bing behind ChatGPT search and Copilot, Google behind Gemini, AI Overviews and AI Mode, and own or partner indexes for Perplexity. The second is licensed data vendors reached through connectors (Morningstar, FactSet, LSEG, S&P). The third is heavily cited third-party domains such as Reddit, YouTube, Wikipedia, Reuters, AP and the FT. Finance answers lean unusually hard on brand-managed sources (Yext: 88% of finance citations), so crawlable, current, consistent first-party fund pages matter more here than in most sectors. Evidence for llms.txt and for FAQ schema on its own is weak. The evidence favours crawlable HTML, visible question-and-answer structure, AI crawlers that are not blocked, and consistent identifiers.

### Cited Findings
**How assistants source information**
- **ChatGPT search** uses third-party search providers plus content supplied directly by partners. — [OpenAI, Introducing ChatGPT search, Oct 2024 (primary text; not re-fetched)](https://openai.com/index/introducing-chatgpt-search/)
- **OpenAI crawlers are separate.** OAI-SearchBot surfaces sites in ChatGPT search, GPTBot collects training data, and ChatGPT-User makes user-initiated fetches. A site can allow search while blocking training. — [OpenAI bots documentation (primary text; not re-fetched)](https://platform.openai.com/docs/bots)
- **Google** says appearing in AI Overviews and AI Mode needs only normal Search eligibility, with no special markup. — [Google Search Central, AI features and your website (primary text; not re-fetched)](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)
- **Little overlap with Google.** Only 6.82% of ChatGPT results overlap with Google's top-10 organic results (Ahrefs, 2025). — [The Digital Bloom](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/ai-citation-position-revenue-report-2026/)
- **OtterlyAI citations report 2026** (more than 1 million citations, Jan–Feb 2026). Community platforms (Reddit, Quora) captured 52.5% of citations against 47.5% for brand domains. **73% of sites have technical barriers that block AI crawler access.** — [Otterly.AI](https://otterly.ai/blog/the-ai-citations-report-2026/)
- **Finance specifically.** Yext's October 2025 research found that **88% of finance citations come from brand-managed sources**. — [AI Growth Agent](https://aigrowthagent.co/articles/ai-search-visibility-financial-brands/) and [Onely](https://www.onely.com/blog/how-investment-companies-can-get-cited-in-ai-answers/) (attribution as summarised; the original Yext study was not retrieved)
- **"Wealth Management AI Study"** (March 2026; 201,233 citations for US adviser-search prompts). Directory and comparison sites led: NerdWallet 38.0% and Bankrate 35.3% of citation share for national "best financial advisor" prompts. — [OpenLens](https://openlens.com/blog/en/ai-visibility-financial-advisors-2026) (as summarised)
- **The citation mix varies by engine and over time.**
  - A 30-million-source analysis puts Reddit first across ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and AI Overviews.
  - A 2026 Ahrefs US dataset has Reddit at 29.4% of tracked citation mentions against Wikipedia at 15.0%. Wikipedia led in Profound's Aug 2024–Jun 2025 data.
  - In a June 2026 Ahrefs snapshot of Perplexity, YouTube had 32.4% of top-50 mention share against Reddit's 16.6%.
  - A 366,087-citation study found **Reuters, AP News and the Financial Times lead OpenAI's publisher citation share**.
  - For "who to hire" queries, directories and review sites dominated: Perplexity 73.0%, Gemini 59.9%, ChatGPT 55.3%.
  - Sources: [Contently, 29 Apr 2026](https://contently.com/2026/04/29/top-sources-llms-cite/); [Everything-PR citation index](https://everything-pr.com/ai-platform-citation-source-index-2026); [AuthorityTech](https://authoritytech.io/curated/which-publications-get-cited-ai-search-engines-2026); [UX Continuum](https://uxcontinuum.com/research/ai-answer-sources-2026); [Sanbi 120K-citation study](https://sanbi.ai/blog/ai-engine-citation-trends-source-affinity-reddit). All are vendor analyses, and individual figures are attributed as extracted.
- **Academic model of brand visibility in AI search** (September 2026). Content not retrieved. — [arXiv 2609.23162, "From Prompt to Recommendation"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23162)

**What helps a page get cited**
- **FAQs.** An SE Ranking analysis found that visible FAQ sections and question-style headings raised citation likelihood, especially for smaller sites. FAQ *schema* on its own had no measurable effect. — [Onely](https://www.onely.com/blog/how-investment-companies-can-get-cited-in-ai-answers/)
- **One citation is a weak signal.** An analysis of 83,633 AI citations over eight weeks across 2,000+ domains (personal-loan queries) found that sustained content work matters more. — [Profundcom](https://profundcom.net/so-your-fund-got-cited-by-chatgpt-once-new-data-shows-that-will-not-matter/)
- **llms.txt.**
  - SE Ranking (about 300,000 domains) found roughly 10.13% adoption and no statistically significant link between having the file and being cited.
  - An OtterlyAI test found 84 requests to llms.txt, 0.1% of AI-crawler traffic. Profound reported Microsoft and OpenAI crawlers fetching llms.txt and llms-full.txt.
  - No major lab had publicly committed to using it as of Q1 2026, and Google has said it does not use it.
  - Sources warn that much 2026 content about llms.txt is AI-generated and contains invented "vendor confirmations".
  - Sources: [Limy](https://limy.ai/blog/llms-txt-in-2026-the-full-guide); [1ClickReport](https://www.1clickreport.com/blog/llms-txt-evidence-2026); [Polaris](https://www.polarisagency.com/marketing-insights/what-is-llms-txt/); [Digital Strategy Force](https://digitalstrategyforce.com/journal/does-your-site-need-llms-txt-to-get-cited-by-ai-search-in-2026/). Origin of the proposal: [llmstxt.org (primary)](https://llmstxt.org/)
- **schema.org InvestmentFund.** schema.org defines an `InvestmentFund` type (under InvestmentOrDeposit and FinancialProduct). Identifiers such as ISIN can be given through generic `identifier`/PropertyValue. — [schema.org/InvestmentFund (primary text; not re-fetched)](https://schema.org/InvestmentFund)
- **Fund-specific GEO guidance exists, but it is vendor or PR content.** — [Everything-PR, hedge fund visibility playbook](https://everything-pr.com/the-hedge-fund-visibility-playbook-how-funds-get-cited-inside-chatgpt-and-perplexity); [Everything-PR, 25 financial-trust prompts across 5 engines, 125 outputs, "directional, not statistically rigorous"](https://everything-pr.com/chatgpt-financial-trust-25-prompts-5-engines/); [Onely, how investment companies get cited](https://www.onely.com/blog/how-investment-companies-can-get-cited-in-ai-answers/)

### Inferences
**GEO playbook for a UK boutique UCITS manager, ranked by expected impact (my synthesis)**
1. **Make the fund facts crawlable.**
   - Give each share class one canonical HTML page keyed by ISIN, carrying SEDOL, the fund and ManCo LEIs, IA and Morningstar category, OCF, launch date, benchmark, PMs with start dates, and NAV with an explicit date.
   - Keep the factsheet, KID/KIID, prospectus and SFDR documents linked from the page, not PDF-only.
   - Publish a sitemap with `lastmod` and a downloadable fund-facts JSON/CSV.
   - Add schema.org `InvestmentFund` JSON-LD. It is mainly useful for entity disambiguation, since schema alone did not lift citations in the SE Ranking analysis.
2. **Fix the professional-investor gate.** The 73% "technical barriers" figure suggests click-through investor-type gates, cookie walls and bot protection often remove funds from AI answers without anyone noticing.
   - Allow search and user-fetch bots in robots.txt and the web application firewall: OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, Bingbot, Googlebot, PerplexityBot and Anthropic's bots. Decide separately whether to block training bots such as GPTBot and Google-Extended.
   - Build two tiers: a public, retail-compliant, country-aware factual layer, and professional-only commentary behind the gate. For EU countries where a fund is not notified for marketing, the public layer must not become marketing.
3. **Index for Bing first.** ChatGPT search and Copilot rely on Bing (the Bing role in ChatGPT search is long reported; **[PRIOR KNOWLEDGE, UNVERIFIED]** for 2026). Use Bing Webmaster Tools and IndexNow to push NAV and factsheet updates fast, and check Bing for stale fund names or share classes.
4. **Keep data consistent across vendors.** AI answers are often grounded in Morningstar, FE or LSEG data. Any mismatch with the firm's own site (OCF, launch date, benchmark, manager names, old fund names after a rename) creates ambiguity that models resolve unpredictably. Reconcile vendor records monthly.
5. **Use visible Q&A blocks on fund pages**, such as "What does the fund invest in?", "How is it different from peers?", "Who runs it and since when?", "Main risks?" and "Charges?". This is supported by the SE Ranking finding on visible FAQs.
   - A new angle: publish an **"identity and disambiguation" page** covering renamed or merged funds, similarly named funds from other managers, and share-class conventions. Such pages are easily retrieved, and they cut down the share-class and name mix-ups behind many AI errors.
6. **Build the third-party footprint that AI engines cite.**
   - PM explainer videos on YouTube with accurate transcripts and chapters. Perplexity and Gemini cite YouTube heavily.
   - Earned media in outlets AI engines over-cite, such as Reuters and the FT.
   - A Wikipedia article on the firm only if it is genuinely notable, and only under Wikipedia's conflict-of-interest rules.
   - Monitor Reddit; never seed it. Seeding would be a financial-promotion and platform-rules risk.
7. **Measure "share of model" with a fixed prompt panel.**
   - Write 50–100 selector-style prompts in EN/DE/FR/IT/ES, for example "best global equity income UCITS for a UK MPS" or "compare [fund] with [peer]".
   - Run them across ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini/AI Overviews, Perplexity and Claude using Profound, Peec, Otterly, Scrunch or the Semrush and Ahrefs tools.
   - Track mention rate, accuracy (correct ISIN, OCF, manager, correctly labelled performance periods), sentiment and the sources cited.
   - Also test enterprise-grounded tools such as Claude or ChatGPT with the Morningstar connector, and Morningstar Direct AI. Those are what selectors actually use, and consumer-facing GEO tools do not cover them.
8. **Reduce hallucinated performance.** Publish month-end performance tables in HTML: standard cumulative periods, **discrete 12-month periods** and the benchmark, each with an as-of date. Models then retrieve numbers rather than guess them, and the presentation already matches past-performance rules.
9. **Use European distributor data templates.** **[PRIOR KNOWLEDGE, UNVERIFIED]** The FinDatEx templates (EMT target market, EET ESG data, EPT PRIIPs data; [findatex.eu](https://findatex.eu/)) are the structured feeds European bank and insurer distributors load into their systems. As distributors add AI agents to those systems, the accuracy of these files becomes another form of AI visibility.

### Gaps
- Not confirmed from primary sources this session: which search providers ChatGPT search uses in 2026; whether Claude's web search still relies on Brave (reported in 2025, **[PRIOR KNOWLEDGE, UNVERIFIED]**); whether Bing Webmaster Tools now reports Copilot or AI-answer citations; Google's current AI-features guidance wording.
- No fund-specific GEO study, no asset-manager GEO case study with results, and no evidence on how assistants handle share-class ambiguity or performance figures.

## 3. Agent-accessible fund data: MCP servers, APIs and AI connectors, and how an asset manager gets its data into fund buyers' AI tools

### Takeaway
By October 2026 the main fund-data vendors can be reached from the AI tools professionals use:
- Morningstar runs an official remote MCP server that its documentation says works with Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot Studio and the Copilot app, Microsoft Foundry and Perplexity.
- Morningstar, LSEG, FactSet, S&P Global and Moody's are connectors in Claude, ChatGPT and Microsoft 365 Copilot (Excel).
- In the US, two asset managers, BlackRock and Vanguard, have connectors inside Anthropic's "Claude for Financial Advisors" (around August–September 2026).

At least one UK boutique, Guinness Global Investors, already runs a public, read-only MCP server for its own fund performance data. I found no UK or European asset manager, and no UK adviser-tech connector, listed in the Claude or ChatGPT directories. That suggests a first-mover window in the UK and EU, though it is not confirmed.

### Inferences
**How a boutique gets its fund data into buyers' AI tools, ranked by leverage**
1. **Indirect, through the vendors (highest reach, lowest effort).** Treat Morningstar, FE fundinfo, LSEG/Lipper, FactSet and S&P as the firm's AI distribution layer. Keep fund records, uploaded documents (factsheets, commentaries, DDQs where the vendor supports them), manager bios, holdings frequency and classifications complete and current. One Morningstar record now reaches Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot Studio, Excel Copilot, Foundry, Perplexity and Morningstar Direct AI.
2. **Direct: a public, read-only "published facts" MCP server, following the Guinness pattern.**
   - Restrict it to data already approved and published, such as month-end performance and NAVs once live on the website.
   - Have every response carry, as structured fields: as-of date, currency, share-class ISIN, data source, standard warnings ("past performance is not a reliable indicator of future results", currency risk), and links to the KID/KIID, factsheet and prospectus.
   - Write tool descriptions that tell the model how to present results, for example "always show discrete 12-month returns alongside cumulative ones; always show the benchmark".
   - Mark the tools read-only.
   - Expose an "approved narrative" tool that returns the compliance-approved strategy description and latest commentary, so AI explanations quote approved text rather than paraphrasing it.
3. **Professional-only due-diligence connector (new angle).**
   - Put DDQ answers, team and process descriptions, lagged holdings and attribution behind OAuth.
   - Verify users by firm, for example against the FCA register or an LEI.
   - Selectors can then plug the manager into Claude, Copilot or ChatGPT and question it during research. That cuts DDQ turnaround, and the usage logs become first-party intent data (see Q6).
4. **Distribution routes.** The mechanics below are **[PRIOR KNOWLEDGE, UNVERIFIED]** except where cited above; check current plan eligibility.
   - **Claude:** users can add a remote MCP URL as a custom connector, so publish the URL and setup instructions. Apply for a Connectors Directory listing; BlackRock's listing shows asset managers are accepted.
   - **ChatGPT:** the Apps SDK (built on MCP) and app directory, plus admin-enabled custom connectors in Business and Enterprise.
   - **Microsoft:** MCP tools in Copilot Studio, packaged as an agent, or a Copilot connector that client IT can approve.
   - **Gemini Enterprise:** check its connector options.
5. **Compliance by design.** LLM clients can drop the context around data. So embed warnings in the data fields, gate EU jurisdictions where funds are not notified, log every response for record-keeping, and version the tool descriptions under compliance sign-off.

- **EU AI Act text** — [EUR-Lex, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (primary text; not re-fetched)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj):
  - **Art. 50(4):** deployers of AI that generates or manipulates image, audio or video "deep fakes" must disclose that the content is artificial.
  - **Art. 3(60):** defines a deep fake as content that resembles existing persons and would falsely appear authentic.
  - **Art. 50(2):** providers must mark synthetic outputs in a machine-readable way.
  - **Art. 2:** the Act applies to non-EU deployers where the output is used in the EU.
  - **Art. 99:** fines for Article 50 breaches of up to €15m or 3% of worldwide turnover.

### Inferences
**Creative techniques with compliant guardrails (my synthesis; to be cross-checked by the compliance researcher)**
1. **Multilingual PM video for European selectors.**
   - **Workflow:** the real PM records the commentary in English; AI dubbing or lip-sync tools (ElevenLabs, HeyGen, Rask or Synthesia) produce DE/FR/IT/ES versions; a native-speaking investment professional and compliance review each version, because terms like "thesaurierend/ausschüttend" and "capitalisation/distribution" must be right.
   - **Disclosure:** a lip-synced or voice-cloned PM very likely meets the Art. 3(60) "deep fake" definition. Under Art. 50(4), label it on screen and in the description, for example: "AI-generated translation of [PM]'s original English recording of [date]".
   - **Safeguards:** attach C2PA content credentials; keep the original video; get the PM's written consent and a likeness and voice licence that ends when they leave the firm.
   - **UK:** there is no equivalent statute, but the FCA and ASA "not misleading" standards make the same disclosure sensible.
   - **Payoff:** local-language video and transcripts also feed local-language AI answers (GEO).
2. **Data-driven or dynamic creative with live fund data.**
   - Feed templates only from approved month-end datasets (for example the firm's own MCP or data feed), never intraday NAVs.
   - Lock the warning text and run a rules engine that enforces standard periods, **discrete 12-month periods**, five years or since launch where shorter (UK retail, COBS 4.6.2R), the benchmark, the source and the as-of date ([FCA Handbook COBS 4.6, primary text; not re-fetched](https://www.handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/COBS/4/6.html)). For EU marketing communications, apply ESMA's cross-border marketing-communications guidelines on past performance, which are consistent with the KID (**[PRIOR KNOWLEDGE, UNVERIFIED]** on paragraph detail).
   - Never show performance only when it looks good. Selective triggering is cherry-picking and fails the "fair, clear and not misleading" test.
3. **Event-triggered creative.**
   - Build pre-approved *context* variants, for example "Oil spike: what it means for energy equities, a 3-minute PM note", that a market-data trigger (sector index move greater than X%) switches on through dynamic creative or LinkedIn.
   - Triggers may change *which approved message runs*, never the claims inside it. Avoid performance figures in triggered variants, and log which variant ran and when.
   - For B2B, the most valuable trigger is often existing holders, for example platform-held positions where known (servicing rather than promotion).
4. **Personalised video at scale for ABM.** A human salesperson records the core message, and AI adds personalised segments (firm name, relevant strategy, meeting call to action). Keep them free of advice or suitability content, label any synthetic segment, and document a legitimate-interest assessment for the personal data used.
5. **AI imagery.** Avoid photorealistic synthetic "team members" or "clients". Label photorealistic AI images, and keep the PM's real likeness unless consent and labelling are in place.

## 7. Key compliance implications (brief; another researcher covers this in depth)

### Takeaway
The AI era changes three things:
1. **Ad-platform gates.** Google and Microsoft require FCA-verified advertiser status in the UK. OpenAI puts investing in an approval-only tier and says financial-services ads outside the US are "generally prohibited".
2. **EU AI Act Article 50 transparency** (from 2 August 2026) for synthetic PM video and voice aimed at EU audiences.
3. **Machine-to-machine distribution.** MCP servers, connectors and crawlable fund pages can lose the warnings and context that past-performance and "fair, clear and not misleading" rules require once an AI client relays them. Compliance therefore has to be built into the data itself.

### Cited Findings
- **Platform gates.**
  - Microsoft UK FS verification. — [Microsoft Advertising](https://about.ads.microsoft.com/en/blog/post/december-2022/verification-of-financial-service-advertisers-in-the-uk?feed=blogposts)
  - Google UK FS verification. — [Google](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/15332527?hl=en&co=GENIE.CountryCode%3DGB)
  - OpenAI licensure, approval and outside-US restrictions. — [OpenAI Ad policies](https://openai.com/policies/ad-policies/); [OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001534-troubleshooting-common-onboarding-and-policy-issues)
- **AI Act Article 50 timing unchanged by the Omnibus**, with a marking grace period for legacy systems to 2 Dec 2026. — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [Tech Jacks Solutions](https://techjacksolutions.com/ai-brief/eu-ai-act-article-50-august-2-annex-iii-december-2027/)
- **No advice from AI data providers.** OpenAI's Financial Services Terms say it does not provide investment advice, and the BlackRock Claude connector disclaims advice and trading. These are examples of disclaimers attached to AI data access. — [OpenAI](https://openai.com/policies/financial-services-terms/); [Claude](https://claude.com/marketplace/connectors/blackrock-advisor-center)
- **Observed MCP design.** A public asset-manager MCP endpoint limited to website-published data returns performance without embedded warnings. — direct inspection of the GuinnessGI MCP server, 27 Oct 2026 (see Q3)
- **UK past-performance conditions** (retail communications). — [FCA COBS 4.6 (primary text; not re-fetched)](https://www.handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/COBS/4/6.html)
- **ESMA (May 2024).** MiFID II obligations apply in full when firms use AI, the management body is responsible, and firms must be transparent about AI use in client interactions. — [ESMA (primary text; not re-fetched)](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-provides-guidance-firms-using-artificial-intelligence-investment-services)

### Inferences
- **Treat AI-facing outputs as communications.** MCP and connector outputs, AI-optimised fund pages and AI-assistant transcripts should go through the financial-promotion approval and record-keeping framework.
  - Embed warnings and context as data fields, and restrict public endpoints to published data.
  - Use OAuth and country gating for professional-only and EU non-notified content.
  - Version the tool descriptions under compliance sign-off, and log what was returned.
- **Turn off unapproved automation.** In ad accounts, disable or tightly control auto-generated copy and URL expansion (Google AI Max text customisation; Microsoft auto-generated assets are already opt-in for FS). The firm remains responsible for any promotion, however it was generated.
- **Retail spill-over.** ChatGPT, Copilot and Google ads cannot be limited to professionals. A UCITS fund ad there is a retail promotion: UK COBS 4 retail rules apply, and in the EU the fund must be notified for marketing in each targeted country (UCITS Directive Art. 93, **[PRIOR KNOWLEDGE, UNVERIFIED]**) and meet ESMA's marketing-communication guidelines.
- **Synthetic media.** Get PM consent and a likeness licence, label clearly (Art. 50(4) for EU audiences; the "not misleading" standard in the UK), and keep originals and provenance data. Check that AI-video vendors can show Art. 50(2) marking by 2 Dec 2026.
