# How TikTok's For You Feed Learns a Brand-New Account: Cold Start, Signal Weights, Learning Speed, and Creator-Side Effects

So every finding below comes from search-engine extracts of those pages, plus secondary coverage. A quote marked "per extract" uses the wording the search extract gave. Re-check exact wording against the original before quoting it in a final report.
> **Evidence tags:** **[Official]** = a TikTok or ByteDance statement. **[Leak]** = an internal document reported by the press. **[Measured]** = an audit or data study with a stated method. **[Secondary]** = a press summary of a measured study whose original I could not reach. **[Claim]** = a creator, vendor or marketing assertion with no published evidence.

## 1. What happens at signup/onboarding, and how do the first sessions shape the feed?

### Takeaway
At signup, TikTok asks new users to pick interest categories, but this step is optional. Anyone who skips it gets a general feed of popular videos (popular and moderated, per the WSJ), shaped by language, region and device settings. TikTok calls those settings weak signals. Personalisation starts as soon as the first likes, comments and replays arrive. Lingering on videos also counts, according to audits. Audits show the feed visibly tilting toward an interest within the first session, in minutes rather than days.

### Cited Findings
- **[Official, 18 Jun 2020]** TikTok's "How TikTok recommends videos #ForYou" explainer says new users are asked to pick categories of interest, "like pets or travel", to tailor their first recommendations. Users who don't pick any are shown "a generalized feed of popular videos" until the system has more input. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you); TechCrunch reported the same mechanics at the time. — [TechCrunch, 18 Jun 2020](https://techcrunch.com/2020/06/18/tiktok-explains-how-the-recommendation-system-behind-its-for-you-feed-works)
- **[Official, 2020]** The first likes, comments and replays "initiate an early round of recommendations" as the system starts learning the user's tastes (per extract). — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)
- **[Official, 2020]** The FYF "recommends content by ranking videos based on a combination of factors, starting from interests you express as a new user and adjusting for things you indicate you're not interested in" (per extract). — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)
- **[Official, current Help Center, undated]** Users who skip interest selection are first shown recent posts that are popular with others. This first set also reflects their location and language settings (per search-result summary of the page). — [TikTok Support: How TikTok recommends content](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/exploring-videos/how-tiktok-recommends-content)
- **[Official, 2020; not fully re-verified in this session]** Device and account settings (language preference, country setting, device type) are inputs, but they carry less weight than interactions. TikTok's example of a weak signal is "whether the video's viewer and creator are both in the same country". — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you); the strong-versus-weak example is also quoted by [Hootsuite (2026 explainer)](https://blog.hootsuite.com/tiktok-algorithm/)
- **[Secondary → Measured, WSJ "Inside TikTok's Algorithm", 21 Jul 2021]** For new users who give little information about themselves, the app showed "a wide array of highly popular, moderated videos" while it worked out what they wanted. As it learned, videos became "more specific and niche, often with fewer views and less oversight". — [Vision Times summary of WSJ](https://www.visiontimes.com/2021/07/24/tiktok-algorithm-grooming-rabbit-hole.html); [5Rights summary](https://5rightsfoundation.com/resource/tiktoks-secretive-algorithm-unpacked-by-the-wall-street-journal/)
- **[Secondary → Measured, WSJ 2021]** TikTok identified most of the 100+ test accounts' interests in under two hours, and some in about 40 minutes. — [Vision Times](https://www.visiontimes.com/2021/07/24/tiktok-algorithm-grooming-rabbit-hole.html); [Digital Information World](https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2021/07/to-test-algorithm-of-tiktok-wsj-created.html)
- **[Official, Dec 2022]** The "Why this video" panel launched for the For You feed on 20 Dec 2022. Its reason categories are the user's previous interactions, accounts the user follows, content posted recently, and content growing popular in the user's region. Recency and regional popularity are therefore explicit sources of recommendations, and they are the main ones available before a profile exists. — [Axios, 20 Dec 2022](https://www.axios.com/2022/12/20/tiktok-explain-video-recommendations-algorithm)
- **[Official, 4 Aug 2023, EU only]** To comply with the DSA, EU users can switch off personalisation, effective from 28 Aug 2023. Their For You and LIVE feeds then show "popular videos from both the places where they live and around the world", and search returns popular regional content in their language. This is effectively TikTok's official description of a feed with no profile behind it. — [TechCrunch, 4 Aug 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/04/tiktok-algorithm-dsa/); [Music Ally, 7 Aug 2023](https://musically.com/2023/08/07/tiktok-will-soon-let-european-users-de-personalise-its-feeds/)
- **[Official, Feb–Mar 2023]** "Refresh your For You feed" was tested in Feb 2023 and rolled out to all users from about 16 Mar 2023; Canada got it later. It lets people view the FYF "as if they just signed up for TikTok", after which recommendations rebuild from new interactions. It does not override content settings already chosen, and it does not affect followed accounts. The path is Settings → Content preferences → Refresh your For You feed. — [TechCrunch, 2 Feb 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/02/02/tiktok-introduces-a-strike-system-for-violations-tests-a-feature-to-refresh-the-for-you-feed/); [9to5Mac, 16 Mar 2023](https://9to5mac.com/2023/03/16/tiktok-rolling-out-for-you-feed-reset-button/); [Ada Derana Biz](https://bizenglish.adaderana.lk/tiktok-introduces-a-new-way-to-refresh-for-you-feed-recommendations/); [Later](https://later.com/blog/tiktok-refresh/); [MobileSyrup, 16 Mar 2023](https://mobilesyrup.com/2023/03/16/tiktok-refresh-for-you-feed-canada/)
- **[Measured, DCU Anti-Bullying Centre, Apr 2024]** Male-identified test accounts on TikTok received masculinist and anti-feminist content within their first 23 minutes, whether or not they had searched for it. This suggests that inferred demographics and early dwell behaviour shape the cold-start feed. — [DCU Institute of Education](https://www.dcu.ie/instituteofeducation/news/2024/apr/new-research-shows-how-tiktok-and-youtube-shorts-are-bombarding)
- **[Measured, Washington Post data donation, 10 Dec 2025]** A topic map built from about 1,100 users' watch histories found that gender has a large effect on which videos people are shown. — [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/interactive/2025/tiktok-algorithm-video-map-interests/)

### Inferences
- A new account's first candidate pool is likely: the chosen categories (if any), plus popular and recent videos for its region and language, plus inferred demographics. Choosing accurate interest categories and setting language and region correctly probably gives a small head start. But TikTok says these are weaker than behaviour within the session.
- TikTok says early personalisation is triggered by the first explicit interactions and replays, so the first session or two is when a new user has the most leverage. The WSJ and DCU results put that tilt at roughly 20–120 minutes of viewing.
- "Refresh your For You feed" is the officially supported way to restart from scratch without creating another account.

### Gaps
- I could not verify the current (2025–2026) onboarding screen: which categories it offers, and whether it appears in every region and age group. TikTok's own pages were blocked in this session.
- TikTok publishes no figure for how many interactions or videos the "early round" of recommendations needs.
- I found no verified information on whether contact syncing or linked social accounts seed a new account's FYF. The "Why this video" categories in the Axios report don't mention them.

## 2. What does TikTok officially say about recommendation signals and user controls?

### Takeaway
TikTok officially groups its signals into three kinds. User interactions include likes, shares, comments, follows, content you create, watching in full versus skipping, and replays. Content information includes captions, sounds and hashtags. User or device information includes language, location and device. TikTok says strong signals, such as finishing a longer video, outweigh weak ones, such as being in the same country. It has never published numerical weights. Leaked and official ByteDance formulas show the same structure: a score built from the predicted probability of each action times a hidden value weight. Since 2023, TikTok has added explicit steering controls on top of that.

### Cited Findings
- **[Official, 2020]** The user interactions TikTok names include "the videos you like or share, accounts you follow, comments you post, and content you create". Video information, such as captions, sounds and hashtags, and device or account settings are also inputs (per extract). — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)
- **[Official, 2020]** Factors are weighted by their value to the user. "A strong indicator of interest, such as whether a user finishes watching a longer video from beginning to end, would receive greater weight than a weak indicator, such as whether the video's viewer and creator are both in the same country" (wording as quoted by secondary sources). — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you); secondary quotes: [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/tiktok-algorithm/), [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/tiktok-ads/tiktok-algorithm-for-you-feed/)
- **[Official, 2020]** "neither follower count nor whether the account has had previous high-performing videos are direct factors in the recommendation system." TikTok does acknowledge that bigger accounts get more views indirectly. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you); [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/tiktok-ads/tiktok-algorithm-for-you-feed/)
- **[Official, 2020]** TikTok acknowledges the "filter bubble" risk, saying that catering too much to personal taste can produce an "increasingly homogeneous stream of videos". — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)
- **[Official, current Help Center]** TikTok names three main factors: User interactions, Content information and User information. It says the importance of each factor can change over time. The interactions it cites include likes, shares, comments, and whether you watch videos in full or skip them (per extract). — [TikTok Support](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/exploring-videos/how-tiktok-recommends-content)
- **[Official → absence]** TikTok has never published numerical weights. Commonly repeated figures, such as watch time or completion being "40–50%" of ranking, or a "70% completion" threshold for going viral, are practitioner estimates with no source. — [FastClips myths explainer (2026)](https://fastclips.app/learn/tiktok-algorithm-myths); [Meikuio (2026)](https://meikuio.com/tiktok-algorithm-2026/)
- **[Leak, NYT/Ben Smith, 5 Dec 2021]** The internal "TikTok Algo 101" document was written for non-technical staff, and TikTok confirmed it was authentic. It gives a "highly simplified" scoring equation: **Plike×Vlike + Pcomment×Vcomment + Eplaytime×Vplaytime + Pplay×Vplay**. Each term is the predicted probability or expected value of an action times a value weight, and the highest-scoring videos are served. The document lists four values (user value, long-term user value, creator value, platform value) and says the system optimises for "retention" (whether users come back) and "time spent". The NYT did not publish the document, and Gizmodo could not verify it independently. — [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/leaked-tiktok-doc-reveals-its-obvious-secret-to-an-addi-1848166901); [GIGAZINE](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20211208-tiktok-algorism); [RouteNote](https://licensing.routenote.com/blog/tiktok-recommendation-algorithm-explained/)
- **[Official, ByteDance/Douyin, 30 Mar 2025]** Douyin, TikTok's Chinese sister app, launched a "Safety and Trust Center" site (95152.douyin.com) that discloses its recommendation principles. Per press coverage, these include a priority score of predicted probability of a user action × that action's value weight, and a two-tower retrieval model. This is the same structure as Algo 101. — [Pandaily](https://pro.pandaily.com/p/douyins-algorithm-transparency-drive); [TechNode, 1 Apr 2025](https://technode.com/2025/04/01/bytedances-douyin-reveals-algorithm-amid-government-push-to-tackle-online-platform-issues/)
- **[Official/technical, ByteDance, Sep 2022]** ByteDance's "Monolith" paper describes a recommender that trains continuously on the live stream of user actions and periodically syncs the updated parameters to the serving system, rather than retraining in batches. The motivation is that user interests change quickly. The sync interval was not in the extracts. — [The Next Platform, 26 Sep 2022](https://www.nextplatform.com/2022/09/26/chinas-bytedance-intros-different-approach-to-recommendation-at-scale/); [paper, CEUR Vol-3303](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3303/paper8.pdf)
- **[Official, Dec 2022] "Why this video"** (see Q1). **[Measured, ICWSM 2024]** Mousavi, Gummadi and Zannettou compared the explanations with users' own GDPR activity data. They found significant discrepancies between the explanations and actual activity, and concluded the feature falls short of meaningful transparency. — [Lacuna summary of the ICWSM 2024 paper](https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/work/auditing-algorithmic-explanations-of-social-media-feeds-a-case-study-of-tiktok/wrk_566f9f0296fc7f048823b1759579cf8e)
- **[Official, 3 Jun 2025] Manage Topics.** Users can set how often content from more than 10 topics is recommended, from Creative Arts to Travel, Nature and Sports, using a slider for each. The path is Settings → Content Preferences → Manage Topics. It doesn't remove a topic entirely, it applies only to the FYF and not to followed accounts, and changes "may take time" to appear. — [TikTok Newsroom, 3 Jun 2025](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/tiktoks-new-features-to-help-you-control-your-scroll); [TikTok Newsroom IE](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-ie/ie-for-you-feed-controls); [Lindsey Gamble](https://www.lindseygamble.com/blog/tiktoks-new-manage-topics-feature-lets-users-fine-tune-their-for-you-feed)
- **[Official, Jun 2025] Smart Keyword Filters.** These use AI to extend an existing keyword filter to similar terms; for example, filtering "remodeling" also filters "renovation". The keyword limit doubled from 100 to 200, and users can see which extra terms are being filtered. — [TechCrunch, 3 Jun 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/03/tiktok-rolls-out-ai-powered-smart-keyword-filters-to-limit-content-you-dont-want-to-see)
- **[Official, Nov 2025]** TikTok added an AI-generated-content slider inside Manage Topics. It can reduce AI content but not switch it off entirely. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-adds-option-to-limit-ai-content-in-your-for-you-feed/805952/); [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/tiktok-ai-transparency/)
- **[Official, 2023] EU non-personalised feed** (see Q1). In its work ahead of Germany's 2025 election, ISD argued that users still have limited control over the data behind their recommendations. — [ISD](https://www.isdglobal.org/digital-dispatch/towards-transparent-recommender-systems-lessons-from-tiktok-research-ahead-of-the-2025-german-federal-election/)
- **[Official/Reported, Jan 2026, US]** Under the TikTok USDS Joint Venture (Oracle, Silver Lake and MGX at 15% each, ByteDance at 19.9%), the algorithm is to be "retrained, tested and updated" solely on US data and held in Oracle's US cloud. The company says users will keep the same experience; Forrester expects users will notice a difference. — [The Register, 23 Jan 2026](https://www.theregister.com/2026/01/23/jv_announces_tiktok_us_acquisition/); [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/tiktok-us-spin-off-finalized-led-by-oracle-silver-lake-and-mgx/); [Forrester](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/the-tale-of-turmoil-ends-us-tiktok-set-to-divest-in-2026/)

### Inferences
- In this P×V structure, each action type (play, length of play, like, comment, and in the full model probably share, follow, save and so on) is a separate prediction target with an undisclosed value weight. Play and playtime terms appear in every leaked or official formula. They are also recorded for every single video served, not just the ones a user acts on. That makes watch behaviour the densest source of evidence for a new account, even where its per-event weight is lower than an explicit action's.
- Official controls work at the level of whole categories or keywords (Manage Topics, keyword filters) or single videos (Not interested). They help push out off-niche topics, but none lets a user name a micro-niche. Specifying a niche still depends on behaviour.
- US users after January 2026 are on a retrained model. Folk rules and audit numbers from before 2026 may not transfer exactly; see the replication caveats in Q3.

### Gaps
- I couldn't access TikTok's current Help Center, Transparency Center or DSA "main parameters" pages. So I couldn't confirm whether saves/favourites, searches, profile visits or shares are named explicitly in today's disclosures, or what "relative importance" wording TikTok gives. A third-party DSA site lists percentage weights for TikTok's factors, but I couldn't confirm them, so treat them as unverified. — [DSA Library](https://dsa-library.com/article/27/)
- Two items in TikTok's earlier statements could not be re-verified in this session. One is the 2020 diversity rule (generally not showing two videos in a row from the same creator or sound). The other is the December 2021 "diversify recommendations" commitments.
- There is no measured evidence yet on how the US retraining from January 2026 has changed learning speed.

## 3. What did independent investigations find about learning speed, and how many videos, minutes or days until the feed converges on a niche?

### Takeaway
When an account signals one narrow interest consistently, the first targeted videos arrive within minutes, in about 2.6–23 minutes. A majority-niche feed takes roughly 20 minutes to 2 hours of focused viewing, which for the WSJ's most-cited example was about 224 videos (≈36 minutes) to reach a 93%-niche feed. Deeper saturation builds over hours to days. Real-user data show the effect keeps compounding over months. Caveats: almost all speed measurements involve sensitive topics and single-minded behaviour, and a 2025 replication found audit findings are poorly reproducible and expire within about three years.

**Validity and drift:**
- **[Measured, Mosnar et al., SIGIR 2025, KInIT]** Re-running earlier TikTok audits about three years later, the authors found "the situation has changed significantly". Reproducibility was poor, and replication took about 9 person-months over 5 months. - **[Measured, Pecher, Bindas et al., arXiv Mar 2026, UMAP 2026, KInIT]** Recommendations drift toward each account's expressed preferences, but the strength depends heavily on the topic. Misinformation-themed content appeared suppressed, while US politics was heavily promoted. Feeds tended to reinforce an account's existing stance, except on US politics, where they leaned toward the opposing stance. — [arXiv 2603.20723](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20723v1); [KInIT](https://kinit.sk/?p=42732)
- **[Official response, 2021]** TikTok told the WSJ that the bots' behaviour isn't representative of real users, because humans have diverse interests. — [Vision Times](https://www.visiontimes.com/2021/07/24/tiktok-algorithm-grooming-rabbit-hole.html); [Marketing Magazine](https://marketingmagazine.com.my/how-tiktoks-algorithm-works-a-fascinating-and-disturbing-analysis)

### Inferences
- **Rough timeline for a person who consistently signals one niche** (synthesised from the table; audits used sensitive topics, so treat these as order-of-magnitude):
  - **First niche videos:** within the first session, about 3–25 minutes (CCDH, Amnesty, DCU).
  - **Niche is the majority of the feed:** about 20 minutes to 2 hours of focused viewing (Amnesty manual accounts, WSJ). In videos, the WSJ's 224 videos in about 36 minutes works out to roughly 6 videos a minute at a bot's fast-skip pace. A human who watches longer will see fewer videos per hour. The order of magnitude is "a few hundred videos, most of them niche-consistent", which matches Northeastern's choice of 200+ watched videos to establish a topic.
  - **Deeper saturation and adjacent sub-niches:** over several hours across sessions, up to several days (Amnesty's 5–6 h, UCL/Kent's 5 days).
  - **Ongoing:** usage and personalisation keep compounding over months (Zannettou; WaPo).
- Even a "converged" feed keeps exploring. The WSJ's extreme single-interest bot still saw 7% non-niche videos, and if Vombatkere's 30–50% exploitation figure holds, real users' feeds are about half exploration. A new user should expect a niche-heavy feed, not a niche-only one.
- Results depend heavily on topic. Sensitive topics may be accelerated or suppressed (KInIT 2026; TikTok's diversification policies). Benign hobby niches have little published timing data, so the timeline above is an extrapolation for them.
- Signals decay, so a niche must be maintained. Topics come back once suppression signals stop (Northeastern), and preferences drift (KInIT).

### Gaps
- I found no published learning curve for a benign hobby niche driven by natural human behaviour. Northeastern's minute-level rebound numbers weren't in accessible extracts.
- I could not access the original WSJ, Boeker & Urman, Vombatkere or KInIT full texts for exact curves; the WSJ figure is 33 or 36 minutes depending on the summary.
- **No Mozilla Foundation or AlgorithmWatch study measuring TikTok's learning speed was found.** Mozilla's user-controls research (Sept 2022) covered YouTube (see Q4). The successor of Tracking Exposed, AI Forensics, appears here only as Amnesty's 2023 technical partner.
- Numbers from CCDH and Amnesty come from advocacy organisations using small account samples, and TikTok has disputed such findings in the past.

## 4. How much do watch time, completion and rewatching matter relative to likes, follows, comments, shares, saves and searches? Do likes and follows shift the feed, and how quickly?

### Takeaway
Implicit watch behaviour is the one signal every source treats as important: dwell time, finishing, and replays. The most recent replication (SIGIR 2025) ranks watching as the strongest personalisation signal, growing with longer or repeated viewing up to a plateau. Earlier audits (WWW 2022, and WWW 2024 as described by the replicators) ranked follows first and likes above watch rate. Likes and follows do shift the feed, but they may first trigger exploration of adjacent content before exploitation. "Not interested" works better than skipping for removing content, but its effect fades. I found no rigorous public data on saves, shares, comments, searches or profile visits.

### Cited Findings
- **[Official, 2020]** Finishing a longer video is TikTok's own example of a "strong indicator". Replays are named alongside likes and comments as what drives the "early round" of recommendations. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)
- **[Leak, 2021]** Algo 101's simplified score uses like, comment, expected playtime and play probability. Share, follow and save terms do not appear in the simplified version. — [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/leaked-tiktok-doc-reveals-its-obvious-secret-to-an-addi-1848166901)
- **[Secondary → Measured, WSJ 2021]** Watch time was the key finding: "TikTok only needs one ... how long you linger over a piece of content." The test accounts expressed interests by rewatching or pausing on related videos. — [Marketing Magazine](https://marketingmagazine.com.my/how-tiktoks-algorithm-works-a-fascinating-and-disturbing-analysis); [9to5Mac, 28 Jul 2021](https://9to5mac.com/2021/07/28/how-tiktoks-algorithm-works/); [Digital Information World](https://www.digitalinformationworld.com/2021/07/to-test-algorithm-of-tiktok-wsj-created.html)
- **[Measured, Boeker & Urman, WWW 2022]** Every tested factor influenced the FYF: language, location, following, liking and watch time. **Follow had the strongest influence, then like, then video view rate.** — [Webis anthology](https://ir.webis.de/anthology/2022.wwwconf_conference-2022.222/); [arXiv 2201.12271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12271)
- **[Measured, Vombatkere et al., WWW 2024]** The paper tested viewing duration, liking and following. According to the 2025 replication's account, it found **follow most impactful**, with **watch giving weaker personalisation, on a par with like**. — [arXiv 2403.12410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12410v1); [arXiv 2504.18140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18140)
- **[Measured, Mosnar et al., SIGIR 2025]** This study found the opposite ranking.
  - **Watching gives the strongest personalisation, and the effect grows when a video is watched longer or several times.** One version of the paper notes that watching more than about 75% of a video does not strengthen the effect further.
  - **Explicit actions (like and follow)** showed "a stronger exploration aspect ... at the start, followed by a strong exploitation phase". Analysing the accounts' GDPR data afterwards raised doubts about how much explicit actions really drive the feed; the extract is truncated at that point.
  - A secondary summary says like and follow "didn't create strong divergence", and that liking gave a personalisation signal only when it was used to define the user's interests.
  — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/3726302.3730293); [arXiv 2504.18140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18140); [KInIT](https://kinit.sk/algorithmic-audits-on-tiktok-why-results-are-hard-to-replicate/)
- **[Measured, CCDH, Dec 2022]** Pausing briefly on and liking videos about one topic produced targeted content within minutes: 2.6 minutes for suicide-related and 8 minutes for eating-disorder content. — [CCDH](https://counterhate.com/research/deadly-by-design/)
- **[Measured, WaPo, Oct 2025]** Just two watched videos on a topic measurably raised that topic's share of the next few hundred videos (+10.7 per 500 for mental health). — [WaPo](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/interactive/2025/tiktok-algorithm-mental-health/)
- **[Measured, Zannettou et al., CHI 2024]** Real users like more videos from accounts they follow than from accounts they don't. Most watch 30–50% of their videos to the end, so finishing is common rather than rare. — [arXiv 2301.04945](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.04945)
- **[Measured, Northeastern, arXiv May 2026, ICWSM 2026]** Accounts were trained on cooking, fitness or sports betting. Two findings:
  - Both "Not interested" and simply swiping past reduced how much of that content was delivered, and **"Not interested" worked better than swiping in most test runs**.
  - **After the accounts stopped signalling, the content came back ("relapse"), more often for accounts that had only skipped.** Even "Not interested" accounts relapsed in some cases (two in cooking and fitness, one in sports betting). Cooking rebounded especially fast; press coverage says within minutes. Effectiveness varied by topic, and the "Not interested" option was described as hard to find in the interface.
  — [Northeastern News](https://news.northeastern.edu/2026/06/09/tiktok-not-interested-algorithm-control/); [TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2026-06-tiktok-tool-swiping-effect-quickly.html); [arXiv 2605.10690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10690); [ICWSM 2026](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/42688); [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/social-media/tiktoks-not-interested-buttons-fails-you-in-merely-a-few-minutes-finds-research/)
- **[Measured, Mozilla Foundation, Sept 2022 — YouTube, not TikTok]** On YouTube, "Not interested" prevented only about 11% of unwanted recommendations, and "Don't recommend channel" about 43%, compared with users who used no controls. I found no Mozilla study of TikTok's controls. — [Mozilla Foundation](https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/blog/mozilla-investigation-youtubes-dislike-button-other-user-controls-largely-fail-to-stop-unwanted-recommendations/)
- **[Measured, DCU, Apr 2024]** Targeted content arrived whether or not the accounts searched, so searching is not necessary for TikTok to infer an interest. — [DCU](https://www.dcu.ie/instituteofeducation/news/2024/apr/new-research-shows-how-tiktok-and-youtube-shorts-are-bombarding)
- **[Official, Jun 2025]** Manage Topics sliders work at the category level, and TikTok warns that changes "may take time". — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/tiktoks-new-features-to-help-you-control-your-scroll)
- **[Claim, 2026, about Douyin]** A marketing agency claims that on Douyin "saves now outrank likes", with evaluation running over seven days. This is not platform documentation and is unverified. — [Lotus Social Agency](https://www.lotussocialagency.com/blog/douyins-2026-algorithm-shift-international-brands)

### Inferences
**Evidence-weighted ranking of ordinary actions for teaching the feed a niche (synthesis):**

| Action | Evidence | Likely effect for a new user |
|---|---|---|
| Watching niche videos through, and rewatching the best ones | Official ("strong indicator"; replays), WSJ, SIGIR 2025 (strongest), Algo 101 playtime terms | **Fastest and most reliable lever.** It is recorded on every video. Gains appear to plateau past about 75% watched (SIGIR 2025), and finishing is common for real users (30–50%), so deliberate rewatches may be the more distinctive signal |
| Swiping off-niche videos quickly | Northeastern (skipping reduces delivery, but more weakly than "Not interested"); official "skip" input | Moderate. It sharpens the signal by contrast, but its effect fades quickly once the behaviour stops |
| Following niche creators | Strongest factor in 2022 and 2024 audits; disputed in 2025 | Measurable, but it may broaden to adjacent content first. It also produces more likes from followed creators (Zannettou) |
| Liking | Second in 2022; weak in 2025 except when it defines an interest; CCDH pausing plus liking gave first exposure in minutes | Moderate. Most useful early, while interests are still being defined |
| Commenting | Official input; separate Pcomment term in Algo 101 | Unknown size. Probably a strong per-event signal, but rare actions are sparse evidence |
| Sharing | Official input ("videos you like or share") | Unknown size. No measurements found |
| Saving / favouriting | No official naming verified; only marketing claims | Unknown |
| Searching | Not verified in official signal lists; DCU shows it isn't required | Plausible but unmeasured. Mainly useful for finding niche videos to watch, which then counts as watch signals |
| Visiting a profile | No evidence found | Unknown |
| "Not interested" | Official control; Northeastern (beats skipping) | Best way to remove off-niche content, but its effect decays and depends on the topic |
| Manage Topics / keyword filters | Official (2022–2025) | Coarse suppression of whole categories or keywords. Changes take time |

- **Do likes and follows shift the feed quickly?** Yes. Every audit that tested them found an effect within the same viewing sessions (Boeker & Urman; CCDH's liking-plus-pausing accounts within minutes). The 2025 replication suggests their first effect may be exploratory, showing related-but-different content before narrowing. So pairing likes and follows with sustained watching is likely more efficient than likes or follows alone.
- Because the studies conflict (2022 and 2024 say follow first; 2025 says watch first) and the system changes over time (KInIT), the safest ranking is to treat sustained watching plus explicit actions on the same niche as complementary. Never rely on explicit taps alone.

### Gaps
- There are no public measured weights for saves, shares, comments, searches or profile visits, and no TikTok document that ranks them.
- The original audits' quantitative tables (Boeker & Urman, Vombatkere, KInIT) were not reachable, so effect sizes can't be quoted.
- Northeastern's exact rebound times and percentages were not in the accessible extracts.

## 5. Must a new account "be in the niche" (have niche viewing history) before posting? What has TikTok said about new videos first going to a small test audience regardless of follower count?

### Takeaway
I found **no official statement and no controlled study** showing that a creator's own viewing history affects how their videos are distributed. TikTok's documented drivers of distribution are the video's own information and viewers' responses. It explicitly says follower count and past hits are not direct factors. TikTok does say that what you create shapes your own For You feed. The "small test audience first, then expansion" model is widely asserted and fits ByteDance's prediction-based design and Douyin's staged "traffic pool" lore, but I found no verifiable TikTok wording for it. Manual "heating" shows that distribution is not purely algorithmic.

### Cited Findings
- **[Official, 2020]** Follower count and previous high-performing videos are "not direct factors"; accounts with more followers get more views only indirectly. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you); [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/tiktok-ads/tiktok-algorithm-for-you-feed/)
- **[Official, 2020]** "Content you create" is one of the user-interaction signals that shape the user's own FYF. — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)
- **[Official, 2020 and current]** "Content information" is one of TikTok's three main recommendation factors; the 2020 explainer gives captions, sounds and hashtags as examples. — [TikTok Support](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/exploring-videos/how-tiktok-recommends-content); [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)
- **[Absence]** Searches for official TikTok wording, a spokesperson statement, or a Creator Academy text saying each new video goes first to a small test group regardless of followers turned up only third-party and creator content. TikTok's support page, as summarised, describes the signals but no initial test audience or its size. — [TikTok Support](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/exploring-videos/how-tiktok-recommends-content)
- **[Claim]** Creators commonly say each video first goes to a test group of "200–500" people and expands if engagement is strong. Some say followers are tested first; one explainer says there is no official proof that videos go to contacts first. — [TikTok Discover compilation of creator posts](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/does-tiktok-show-your-video-to-first); [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/tiktok-ads/tiktok-algorithm-for-you-feed/)
- **[Claim, 2026]** Several 2026 blogs claim TikTok switched to "follower-first" testing. At least one admits this rests on creator observation, not any TikTok announcement. — [SocialDay](https://socialday.live/features/tiktoks-follower-first-algorithm-is-quietly-killing-reach-for-creators-with-dise); [Social Scale Hub](https://www.socialscalehub.com/academy/tiktok-follower-first-algorithm-2026)
- **[Claim, Douyin]** Douyin commentary describes staged distribution, where new content gets initial traffic "as a data reference for the next layer of traffic recommendation", then is "upgrade[d] layer by layer". The "200–500 users" first-pool size comes from a non-official Medium post. Even commercial guides say Douyin never published pool sizes, and Douyin's March 2025 disclosure, as reported, doesn't give one. — [MMG Thailand](https://mmgthailand.com/douyin-tiktoks-decentralized-algorithm/); [Medium](https://medium.com/@zannatykhatun02/xiaohongshu-video-account-douyin-traffic-algorithm-mechanism-recommended-to-collect-f759ceafa688); [Pandaily](https://pro.pandaily.com/p/douyins-algorithm-transparency-drive)
- **[Leak/Reported, Forbes, Jan 2023]** TikTok and ByteDance staff can "heat" chosen videos, manually pushing them into For You feeds to reach set view counts. An internal "MINT Heating Playbook" put heated videos at about **1–2% of total daily video views**. Heating was used to court influencers and brands, and was sometimes misused for staff's own or friends' accounts; one such account got more than 3M views. TikTok said only a few US staff can approve promotion in the US, and that such content is about **0.002% of videos** in For You feeds. That is a share of videos rather than views, so it doesn't directly contradict the 1–2% figure. — [PetaPixel, 23 Jan 2023](https://petapixel.com/2023/01/23/tiktok-admits-its-own-employees-can-decide-what-videos-go-viral/); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/tiktok-has-a-secret-heating-cheating-button-391983)
- **[Claim, vendor "account warm-up" guides, 2025–2026]** Guides found in search say what an account likes, saves and watches to the end decides its audience's niche. Some give specific thresholds, such as 2–3 weeks of activity before posting, or 150–300 views as a sign a throttle has lifted, with no cited data. Some also call "mandatory waiting periods" and "magic post counts" myths. These statements came from search extracts across several such guides and could not be attributed to individual guides; the two cited here are examples of the genre. Several vendors in this space sell multi-account or automation services, which are out of scope and not cited. — [SocialKit](https://socialk.it/en/blog/tiktok-account-warmup-guide); [Cut.pro](https://cut.pro/en/blog/how-to-warm-up-tiktok-account-2026)
- **[Measured, SIGIR 2025]** TikTok audit findings lose validity within about three years, which implies that folk rules built on past behaviour go stale too. — [arXiv 2504.18140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18140)

### Inferences
- **On "be in the niche":** the evidence neither supports nor refutes it.
  - The documented inputs to a video's distribution are its content information and how early viewers respond. Follower count matters only indirectly.
  - For viewing history to matter, TikTok would have to use an account's inferred interest profile as a feature for its videos or for choosing seed viewers. Nothing official or measured shows that. Douyin-style "account labelling" lore asserts it without evidence.
- **What niche viewing does do, demonstrably:** it tunes the creator's own FYF to the niche, because TikTok says interactions and created content shape your feed. That helps a creator learn the niche's formats, sounds and trends, which plausibly improves their content. That is a real benefit that doesn't depend on any distribution effect.
  - Practical framing: niche viewing before posting costs little and helps research, but there is no evidence it is *required* for reach.
- **On test audiences:** a "small sample, then expand on engagement" process is consistent with the P×V design. Scoring a new video needs early engagement data, and TikTok says past performance and followers aren't direct factors, so every video has to earn distribution on its own. It is very likely in some form, but TikTok's exact mechanics are undocumented (size, whether followers or contacts come first, how long it lasts). Heating shows that manual overrides exist for a small share of views.
- Because the US recommender was retrained from January 2026, creator lore that predates it, including the 2026 "follower-first" chatter, should be treated as unverified.

### Gaps
- I found no official TikTok wording, in accessible sources, that explicitly describes an initial test audience or says it applies "regardless of follower count". Only the 2020 statement about follower count and past performance was found.
- I found no controlled experiment comparing reach for new accounts with and without niche viewing history before posting.
- I found no official information on whether a creator's language or region settings, or inferred demographics, restrict who is in a new video's first audience.
