# Published TikTok "warm-up" guides vs folklore (one person, one new account, manual, own phone)

Scope note: I left out the parts of guides that cover multi-account farms, automation tools, VPNs, proxies, SIMs, emulators or device fingerprinting. Affected sources: vpntous (VPN/region setup), Superwarm (automation programme), AI Flow Chat ("new device/IP" last resort), Makeviral (VPN + US App Store), Undetectable/Multilogin/GeeLark (fingerprint tactics), and 360uniquizer (a farm guide, excluded almost entirely).

Method caveat for the report writer: full-page fetches failed in this session. WebFetch returned DNS errors and the egress proxy returned 403 for general web hosts. ## 1. Recommended warm-up durations and the daily activity each guide prescribes (comparison table)

### Takeaway
Published schedules run from "1–2 days as a viewer" to "1–2 weeks". The most common answer is 5–7 days of niche scrolling (about 10–30 min/day) with light, spaced engagement before a first post. Nearly every detailed schedule comes from a vendor selling automation, warm-up, clipping, scheduling or VPN services, or selling accounts. None cites TikTok documentation or measured data, and several say outright that TikTok has published no warm-up rule.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The spread is very wide: 10–15 min/day (Ssemble) versus 1–2 hours (Playkit), and a first post anywhere from day 1 (OneUp, in-app) to day 10 (Conbersa, MonetizedNow). With no source citing data, this spread suggests the numbers are house heuristics rather than measured thresholds.
- Many sources serve multi-account or automation customers, whose accounts share devices or networks and act on scripts. Their risk model probably overstates the need for a ritual for one person on their own phone. This is my inference; no source tests it.
- Across sources, the closest thing to a consensus single-account recipe is about 3–7 days of 10–30 min/day niche viewing, with likes and follows in the single digits per day and at most a few comments, before a first post.

### Gaps
- The search extracts gave no specific numbers for LIVE watching, Explore/Discover browsing, profile visits, shares or saves (apart from Conbersa's "from day 8–10"), or sound/hashtag searches. Only vpntous ("watch and search" on day 1) and Playkit (niche keyword searches on days 3–5) mention searching.
- An "8–12% of watched videos" like rate appeared in the Conbersa/360uniquizer search results, but I could not tie it to a specific page, so it is not in the table.
- A "5x higher chance of views if warmed up" figure appeared in an extract from a growth-tool blog. It had no source and I could not tie it to a specific page.
- Publication dates are missing for most pages (MonetizedNow, ReelFarm, Influencer Marketing Hub, Conbersa, Playkit, SocialKit, Undetectable).
- I found no warm-up guide from a well-known independent creator educator (YouTube or blog) with numbers. A "RAW GAME EP 14" video summary on dicloak.com came up but was not examined.

## 2. How guides ramp activity day by day, and when and how to start posting

### Takeaway
Most guides follow the same order: watch, then like/follow, then comment, then post. They disagree a lot on timing. The first post lands anywhere from day 1 (in-app, per OneUp) to days 8–10 (MonetizedNow, Conbersa), and early cadence runs from one post every two days to three a day by day 6–7. Mainstream data and TikTok's own advice focus on a pace you can sustain, not on a warm-up phase.

#### Mainstream and official frequency guidance (not warm-up-specific)
- Hootsuite's shadowban article quotes expert Vlasceaunu listing "posting new content too often, especially when you first create a TikTok account" as spammy behaviour. She also says jumping back in with 3–4 videos a day after months away may look spammy, and advises "easing back in with consistency first". — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/tiktok-shadowban/)
- Hootsuite recommends 3–5 TikTok posts a week for businesses. It also says TikTok itself recommends 1–4 posts a day. — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/how-often-to-post-on-social-media/)
- TikTok Creator Academy (posting-cadence article, written for publishers) says there is no one-size-fits-all frequency. It advises setting a daily post target you can consistently hit. — [TikTok Creator Academy](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/posting-cadence-best-practices-publishers)
- The "TikTok recommends 1–4 posts per day" figure is widely attributed to TikTok's Creator Portal, but I could only find it in third-party blogs. — [JoinBrands](https://joinbrands.com/blog/how-often-to-post-on-tiktok/); [Sociallyin](https://sociallyin.com/resources/how-often-should-you-post-on-tiktok/)
- Buffer analysed ~11.4M TikTok posts from 150,000+ accounts:
  - Moving from 1 post a week to 2–5 raised views per post by up to 17%; 6–10 a week gave +29% and 11+ a week gave +34%.
  - Median views per post stayed roughly flat (~500). Mainly the chance of a breakout rose: the 90th percentile was ~3,722 views at 1 post/week versus ~14,401 at 11+/week.
  - Buffer sells scheduling software.
  — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-should-you-post-on-tiktok/)
- In Buffer's 30-day experiment, Kirsti Lang posted daily on a small account (~70 followers at the start) and reached ~1,300 followers and 385K views. She found off-topic videos, even popular ones, did little for audience growth. This was not a brand-new account and had no warm-up arm. — [Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/tiktok-experiment/)
- A Hootsuite/Sprout extract advises new accounts to "experiment" with styles and trends. It notes TikTok gauges a video's engagement by showing it to a limited number of users first, and that videos can take ~24 hours to spread. I could not tell which of the two pages each line came from. — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/how-to-get-more-views-on-tiktok/); [Sprout Social](https://sproutsocial.com/insights/how-to-get-more-followers-on-tiktok/)

### Inferences
- Vendor warm-up guides and mainstream frequency advice roughly agree once the first post is out: build gradually to something like 1 post a day, or 2–5 a week. The mainstream sources simply skip the pre-post phase.
- ReelFarm's "150–300 views means the throttle lifted" uses the same numbers creators call "200-view jail" (see section 5). In other words, an ordinary small test audience is being read as proof of a penalty.

### Gaps
- None of the extracts gave posts-per-week figures for weeks 3–4 beyond "build toward daily".
- I could not find TikTok primary documentation for "1–4 per day".
- I found no source with a controlled test of first-post timing (day 1 vs day 7).

## 3. Profile setup advice: when to add photo, bio, username and links; all at once or gradually

### Takeaway
Guides split. One camp keeps the profile blank for 1–3 days and adds it gradually (Superscale, vpntous, MonetizedNow). The other fills the profile at setup (ReelFarm). The only point most agree on is no external links early. I found no TikTok source suggesting profile timing affects reach. The hard platform rules that matter are the 30-day username-change limit and the follower or business-account requirement for a clickable link.

### Cited Findings
- Superscale: keep the profile "completely blank (no bio, no profile pic)" on days 1–3, add bio and picture on days 4–7. It lists "adding full profile info on day one" as a mistake. — [Superscale](https://superscale.ai/learn/the-right-way-to-warm-up-new-tiktok-instagram-accounts/)
- vpntous: don't touch bio or photo on day 1; editing bio and photo is allowed from day 2. — [vpntous](https://vpntous.com/guides/tiktok-warmup)
- MonetizedNow: no bio links on days 1–3. Fill the profile gradually on days 4–7: photo, short bio, niche-fitting username. Check the username first, "because TikTok only lets you change it once every 30 days". — [MonetizedNow](https://monetizednow.com/warm-up-tiktok-account)
- ReelFarm: "create the account and fill in the profile, without posting" at setup, before warm-up. — [ReelFarm](https://reel.farm/guides/how-to-warm-up-tiktok-account)
- Anti-detect/multi-account vendor guides flag adding external links or pushing traffic off-platform early as risky. The extract did not say which of the three pages says this. — [Undetectable](https://undetectable.io/blog/tiktok-account-warm-up/); [Multilogin](https://multilogin.com/blog/how-to-warm-up-tiktok-account/); [GeeLark](https://www.geelark.com/blog/how-to-warm-up-your-tiktok-accounts/)
- TikTok Support confirms the username can be changed only once every 30 days. The display name is separate. — [TikTok Support](https://www.tiktok.com/support/faq_detail?id=7096681481912752642)
- Third-party guides say a clickable website link needs 1,000+ followers or a (registered) business account, and that the feature is not available in every market. — [Rocketlink](https://rocketlink.io/blog/tiktok-link-in-bio-requirements); [Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/blog/how-to-add-link-to-tiktok-bio)
- TikTok's Help Center ranking explanation lists user interactions, video information (captions, sounds, hashtags) and device/account settings, with device/account settings given lower weight. It says nothing about profile completeness or bio timing. — [TikTok Support](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/exploring-videos/how-tiktok-recommends-content)

### Inferences
- "Profile gradually" has no stated mechanism or evidence behind it. It looks borrowed from multi-account and farm practice, where many fresh accounts set up identically are the signal being avoided. For one real person, choosing a username early (given the 30-day limit) and avoiding early link-pushing are the defensible parts.
- Link restrictions mean a personal account usually cannot add a clickable link early anyway, so "don't add links early" partly happens by default.

### Gaps
- I found no TikTok official or Creator Academy page advising when to complete a profile.
- I found no test of a blank profile against a complete one.

## 4. Where guides broadly agree and where they disagree

### Takeaway
They agree on direction: watch before posting, ramp up gradually, stay in a niche, post original non-promotional content first, avoid bursts, reposts and early links. They disagree on almost every number: duration, minutes, engagement counts, comment timing, profile timing and early cadence. They also disagree on whether posting during warm-up is fine (OneUp, SocialKit) or harmful (Ssemble, Cut.Pro).

### Cited Findings

#### Points of broad agreement
- Consume niche content first, then add likes/follows, then comments, then posting. — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/tiktok-account-warm-up/); [Conbersa](https://www.conbersa.ai/learn/how-to-warm-up-tiktok-accounts); [vpntous](https://vpntous.com/guides/tiktok-warmup)
- Avoid bursts and mass actions:
  - Influencer Marketing Hub: big bursts of likes, follows or comments look unnatural.
  - Superscale: don't like every post or mass-follow in one session.
  - AI Flow Chat: don't follow hundreds in a day.
  - Hootsuite's expert: don't overpost right after creating the account.
  — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/tiktok-account-warm-up/); [Superscale](https://superscale.ai/learn/the-right-way-to-warm-up-new-tiktok-instagram-accounts/); [AI Flow Chat](https://aiflowchat.com/blog/articles/get-views-on-a-new-tiktok-account-a-7-day-guide); [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/tiktok-shadowban/)
- First posts should be original and non-promotional; reposting others' videos is a common warning. One anti-detect vendor page (the extract did not say which) notes that TikTok classifies unoriginal or reused content as possibly not eligible for recommendation. — [vpntous](https://vpntous.com/guides/tiktok-warmup); [Conbersa](https://www.conbersa.ai/learn/how-to-warm-up-tiktok-accounts); [Cut.Pro](https://cut.pro/en/blog/how-to-warm-up-tiktok-account-2026); [Ssemble](https://www.ssemble.com/blog/account-warmup-guide-2026); [Undetectable](https://undetectable.io/blog/tiktok-account-warm-up/) / [Multilogin](https://multilogin.com/blog/how-to-warm-up-tiktok-account/) / [GeeLark](https://www.geelark.com/blog/how-to-warm-up-your-tiktok-accounts/)
- Stay in one niche and use the For You feed matching your niche as the readiness signal. — [MonetizedNow](https://monetizednow.com/warm-up-tiktok-account); [Superscale](https://superscale.ai/learn/the-right-way-to-warm-up-new-tiktok-instagram-accounts/); [SocialKit](https://socialk.it/en/blog/tiktok-account-warmup-guide)

### Inferences
- The agreed points (human pace, original content, niche focus, no early link-spam) line up with things TikTok does police: fake engagement, unoriginal content, spam (section 5). The disputed points (exact days, minutes, counts) are exactly where no evidence exists.

## 5. Contested or unverified folklore, and what experts, TikTok and tests say (including warmed vs non-warmed comparisons)

### Takeaway
I found no rigorous, controlled comparison of warmed and non-warmed accounts. The evidence is vendor assertion, forum anecdote, and TikTok's own self-reported statements. TikTok's statements contradict the "your first videos decide your fate forever" family of claims, and describe documented, notified For You feed ineligibility rather than secret account-level shadowbans. The defensible kernel of warm-up is "don't behave like a bot or spammer on a brand-new account". TikTok demonstrably removes fake accounts and fake engagement at scale. The specific numbers, waiting periods and rituals are unverified folklore.

### Cited Findings

#### Official TikTok statements relevant to the folklore
- TikTok Newsroom (June 2020): "a video is likely to receive more views if posted by an account that has more followers… but neither follower count nor whether the account has had previous high-performing videos are direct factors in the recommendation system." — [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you)
- TikTok Help Center:
  - Recommendations rank videos from user interactions, video information, and device/account settings (the last weighted lower).
  - A similarity check stops near-duplicate videos (for example, same sound) from appearing back to back.
  - Videos rising in popularity get a second check for suitability.
  — [TikTok Support](https://support.tiktok.com/en/using-tiktok/exploring-videos/how-tiktok-recommends-content)
- A TikTok spokesperson told Fox News Digital that the platform does not "shadow ban" creators. The spokesperson said action is taken only after repeated guideline violations and the creator is notified. The date was not shown in the extract (it was a story about golfer Paige Spiranac). — [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/sports/paige-spiranac-claims-girls-got-her-shadow-banned-tiktok/); [Make Influence summary](https://www.makeinfluence.com/en/academy/is-there-really-an-instagram-or-tiktok-shadowban)
- TikTok's documented mechanism is For You feed ineligibility. Content can stay up but not be recommended. TikTok says it may notify creators with the reason, show the notice in the post's analytics, and allow an appeal. — [TikTok content violations page](https://www.tiktok.com/en/safety-hc/account-and-user-safety/content-violations-and-bans); [TikTok reporting page](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/tools-and-guides/reporting)

- TikTok removes fake accounts at very large scale: 76,991,660 in Q2 2025 and 147,716,518 in Q4 2025. The Q4 2025 coverage also says removals of fake likes and followers increased. — [HAPA Kenya (Q2 2025)](https://hapakenya.com/2025/11/04/tiktok-q2-2025-report-details-massive-global-content-enforcement/); [Pakistan Today (Q2 2025)](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=215630); [Dhaka Tribune (Q4 2025)](https://www.dhakatribune.com/amp/feature/tech/408119/tiktok-releases-q4-2025-community-guidelines)

The Intercept obtained internal documents telling moderators to keep certain users' content off the For You feed. So suppression fears have some basis, although these episodes have nothing to do with warm-up. — [The Markup (Apr 2021)](https://themarkup.org/working-for-an-algorithm/2021/04/22/shadow-bans-dopamine-hits-and-viral-videos-all-in-the-life-of-tiktok-creators)

#### Research and investigations
- **Klug, Qin, Evans & Kaufman, WebSci '21 ("Trick and Please"):** interviewed 28 users about their folk theories, then tested them against 300,617 trending videos. Engagement and posting time were associated with trending; the belief that trending hashtags or #fyp tags push videos was not supported. — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Trick-and-Please.-A-Mixed-Method-Study-On-User-the-Klug-Qin/ed740d6f7ab3d8053a646512c8c0dc99179f58f0)
- **Klug (AoIR 2022), "Deleting Videos on TikTok as Algorithm Related Impression Management":** 28 US interviews (Aug 2020). Users believed deleting would "mess up" their personalised feed or change how the algorithm profiled their account, so some set videos to private instead. This documents perceptions, not measured effects. — [SPIR/AoIR](https://spir.aoir.org/ojs/index.php/spir/article/view/13036)
- **Delmonaco, Mayworm, Thach, Guberman, Augusta & Haimson (2024, PACM HCI):** marginalised users try to "prove" shadowbans through collaborative "algorithm investigation". It is a qualitative study of beliefs and experiences and does not establish that shadowbans happened. — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/%22What-are-you-doing,-TikTok%22-:-How-Marginalized-and-Delmonaco-Mayworm/832e799fd4d0ea04c593c9b3e284a9200a8eb3fd); [UMSI](https://www.si.umich.edu/about-umsi/news/shadowbanning-some-marginalized-social-media-users-believe-their-content-suppressed)
- **WSJ investigation (July 2021):** used ~100 bot accounts with assigned interests. Watch time (lingering or rewatching) was the decisive signal for learning a viewer's interests, faster than likes or shares, and interests were identified in under two hours. This concerns the viewer's feed, not a creator's reach. — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2021/07/21/tiktok-recommendation-algorithm-wall-street-journal-guillaume-chaslot/); [netzpolitik.org](https://netzpolitik.org/2021/datenjournalismus-tiktoks-algorithmus-draengt-zu-nischeninhalten/)
- **Mosnar et al. (SIGIR 2025):** replicating sock-puppet audits of TikTok's recommender took ~9 person-months over 5 months. Reproducibility and generalisability were poor because of platform and content changes. Even careful bot tests of TikTok go stale quickly, and informal vendor "tests" are weaker still. — [arXiv 2504.18140](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.18140)

### Inferences
- The "consensus" in warm-up content comes from vendors repeating one another, not from independent confirmation. Several have a direct commercial interest in warm-up seeming necessary: they sell warm-up automation, aged or "warmed" accounts, VPNs or anti-detect tools.
- TikTok's official posture (no shadowbans; per-video ranking; notified, appealable ineligibility) is self-reported and cannot be independently verified. The 2020 #BLM glitch and the Intercept moderation documents show TikTok has suppressed content in the past. The fair reading: suppression happens through documented ineligibility and enforcement, and nothing ties it to whether a new account did a warm-up.
- OneUp is a scheduling vendor telling users not to use its own scheduler for the first 7 days. That makes it a slightly more credible signal than the self-serving vendor claims, but it is still anecdotal.
- For one person on their own phone, the evidence supports only mild practices: don't mass-follow, mass-like or mass-comment; don't post a burst of videos or reposts on day 1; post original content; let videos run before judging them. It does not support fixed waiting periods, blank profiles or "watch to the end" as anti-ban requirements.

### Gaps
- No TikTok official statement explicitly about "warming up" an account was found, for or against.
- I found no controlled study, published case study with a control account, or TikTok staff statement comparing warmed and non-warmed accounts.
- No TikTok staff statement on deleting videos or switching niche was found. Searches for creator-facing TikTok staff (e.g., Kudzi Chikumbu) gave only general advice.
- The Fox News spokesperson quote is undated in the extract and seen only secondhand.
- I could not confirm whether the video-level For You ineligibility notices tested in 2023 rolled out to every market.

## 6. Differences for business vs personal accounts, and for UK vs US

### Takeaway
I found no warm-up guide that changes its schedule for business accounts or for UK vs US users. Account-type differences are practical, not algorithmic. The documented one is earlier access to a clickable link for business accounts; the commonly reported commercial-only music limit appeared only in an unattributed extract. The "business accounts get less reach" claim is unconfirmed. Region-specific "advice" turned up only as VPN-based content, which is out of scope.

### Cited Findings
- Circleboom says switching to a business account won't reduce reach on TikTok. The search summary of other account-type comparison guides (Hootsuite, SocialKit, Trengo, Printify and others) also said TikTok has not confirmed any ranking difference by account type, but did not say which page said what. — [Circleboom](https://circleboom.com/blog/do-tiktok-business-accounts-get-less-views/)
- A clickable link requires 1,000+ followers for personal accounts. Business accounts may get one sooner, depending on market and verification, and the feature is not available everywhere. — [Rocketlink](https://rocketlink.io/blog/tiktok-link-in-bio-requirements)
- Hootsuite's posting-frequency guidance for businesses is 3–5 TikTok posts a week. — [Hootsuite](https://blog.hootsuite.com/how-often-to-post-on-social-media/)
- UK-related warm-up content found was VPN-based ("Reach US audiences from the UK"), which is out of scope. — [vpntous UK page](https://vpntous.com/from/united-kingdom)
- TikTok's 2023 For You ineligibility notification was tested only in "select markets", so transparency tools may differ by region. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-launches-new-strikes-based-penalty-system-for-repeat-offenders/641889/)

### Inferences
- For a UK or US single-account user, nothing in the published guidance suggests a different warm-up. The documented differences (link eligibility, music library, rollout of notifications) are platform-feature differences, not reach rules.

- No UK- or US-specific manual warm-up advice was found, and no official TikTok statement on regional differences for new accounts.
