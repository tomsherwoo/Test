# TikTok's official rules, new-creator guidance and activity limits for a new account (UK/US focus)

## 1. What do TikTok's Community Guidelines and Terms of Service say about spam, fake engagement, inauthentic behaviour and engagement manipulation?

### Takeaway
TikTok's current Community Guidelines ban several things: spam, "automation tools, scripts, or other tricks designed to bypass our systems", buying or selling engagement, and asking people to engage (for example "like-for-like" promises). The current version is the 2026H2 update, released 25 Aug 2026 and effective 24 Sep 2026; its core fake-engagement text is word-for-word the same as the 2025H2 version, effective 13 Sep 2025. Penalties range from removing fake likes and followers, through limiting reach in the For You feed (FYF) and search, to banning the account and any new accounts the person creates. The UK/EEA Terms of Service (last updated July 2026) separately prohibit "inauthentic commercial behaviours such as operating spam or impersonation accounts" and automated data extraction that TikTok has not approved. No official text sets numeric activity thresholds. The rules target automated, bulk, repetitive, purchased or solicited engagement, not ordinary manual use.

### Inferences
- Nothing in the official text caps or forbids ordinary, varied, manual activity. The prohibited categories are automation, bulk or repetitive actions, buying or selling engagement, using other accounts to dodge restrictions, and soliciting engagement ("like-for-like", false incentives to follow).
- "Send repetitive content", "a large amount of irrelevant material" and comment spam ("Random text, irrelevant promotions, or links") all describe behaviour patterns, not tools. Copy-pasted or generic comments can therefore look like spam even when typed by hand. This is an inference from the wording.
- TikTok's official text never names follow/unfollow churn, mass liking or mass commenting. These fall under "spam" or "manipulating engagement" only by inference.
- The official response to "suspicious activity" includes verification challenges (SMS or email, CAPTCHA, verified device). A new account doing unusual bursts of activity should expect these before any formal penalty. This is an inference from the Platform Security text.

- I could not retrieve the verbatim text of the current US Terms (Jan 2026 rewrite), so I cannot quote its automation or inauthentic-behaviour clauses.

## 2. What official guidance does TikTok give new creators about getting started (profile setup, engaging with the community, when to start posting, posting frequency and consistency)?

### Takeaway
TikTok's official getting-started guidance is qualitative. It covers completing the profile (personalised username, photo, nickname, short bio), choosing a personal or business account by goal (only personal accounts can earn through Creator Rewards), posting original content, and posting on a consistent cadence you can sustain. The widely quoted "TikTok recommends posting 1–4 times a day" could not be traced to a primary TikTok page. I found no official TikTok guidance that recommends a waiting or "warm-up" period before a new account starts posting.

### Cited Findings
- Creator Academy is TikTok's official free hub covering community guidelines, creation tips and monetisation, with a getting-started section on creating an account and setting up a profile. TikTok introduced it as the replacement for the Creator Portal. — [TikTok Creator Academy](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy); [Creator Academy – Get started](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/articles/get-started/); [TikTok Newsroom: Empowering creators… (Creator Academy replaces Creator Portal)](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/for-creators-future-format-summit)
- Profile setup (official): you can sign up by email, phone number or a third-party platform. The starting username is generic and can be personalised at any time. TikTok suggests uploading a profile picture, picking a nickname and adding a short bio to introduce yourself. — [Creator Academy – Get started](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/articles/get-started/)
- Account type: Creator Academy says the choice between a personal and business account depends on your goals. — [Creator Academy – Get started](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/articles/get-started/). TikTok's official @creatoracademy account (post dated 22 Jun 2026, decoded from the video ID): "Switch to a Personal account first so you stay eligible for Creator Rewards, Subscriptions, and TikTok Shop." — [@creatoracademy video](https://www.tiktok.com/@creatoracademy/video/7654269670391926047). Only personal accounts are eligible for Creator Rewards; business accounts and government, politician or political-party accounts are not. — [TikTok Creator Rewards Program help](https://www.tiktok.com/support/faq_detail?id=7581821550694013452&category=web_account)
- Posting cadence (official, aimed at publishers and brands): there's "no one-size-fits-all" answer. TikTok advises setting a daily post target you can consistently hit and planning content around it, says an "always-on" approach keeps a consistent presence, and says steady output delivers more impact than occasional bursts. — [Creator Academy: Posting cadence best practices (publishers)](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/posting-cadence-best-practices-publishers)
- "1–4 posts per day" attribution: many third-party blogs say TikTok "officially recommends posting 1 to 4 times per day", often citing the old Creator Portal, but none link a primary TikTok page. One blog (JoinBrands) cites the 1–4/day range but also says 1–4 posts per week can work better than daily posting. — [Medium: TikTok posting guide 2026](https://medium.com/@trulydigital/the-ultimate-tiktok-posting-guide-for-2026-exactly-how-many-videos-you-should-post-per-day-to-grow-e625068c83cb); [JoinBrands](https://joinbrands.com/blog/how-often-to-post-on-tiktok); [Vizard](https://vizard.ai/blog/how-often-to-post-on-tiktok)
- Official description of how the FYF learns (relevant to early browsing and engagement): "The system looks at things like what you like, share, comment on, and search for, as well as what's trending." — [TikTok CG, FYF Eligibility Standards](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/fyf-standards) via [OTA capture (2026H2)](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/TikTok/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- Official reassurance for low-view posts: "If a video isn't getting many views, it also doesn't necessarily mean it broke a rule. Creators can check TikTok's analytics to see how their videos are performing, including if there were any made ineligible for recommendation." — [TikTok CG, FYF Eligibility Standards](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/fyf-standards) via [OTA capture](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/TikTok/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- Originality: unoriginal or reused material "without anything new" is FYF-ineligible. — [TikTok CG](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/integrity-authenticity) via [OTA capture](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/TikTok/Community%20Guidelines.md). Creator Academy describes the content it wants as "well-crafted, engaging, and specialized", shown alongside its Creator Rewards criteria. — [TikTok Creator Academy](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy)
- Engaging with others: comments that "don't add to the conversation" (spam, non-neutral profanity, offensive statements) are sorted lower. — [TikTok CG, Accounts & Features](https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/accounts-features) via [OTA capture](https://github.com/OpenTermsArchive/pga-versions/blob/main/TikTok/Community%20Guidelines.md)
- TikTok's @creatoracademy account posts beginner content ("New to TikTok? Don't sweat it! We're here to help you shine…", 14 Mar 2025). — [@creatoracademy video](https://www.tiktok.com/@creatoracademy/video/7481805018836831495)
- Creator advice that is not official: one creator would rather you post once a week for two years than every day for three weeks; others suggest "1 to 3 videos per day", "3–4 posts per week for beginners", hook viewers in the first 3 seconds, and don't open the account with a boring intro video. — creator videos and TikTok Discover pages collected via search, e.g. [TikTok Discover: How often should you post](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/how-often-should-u-post-content?lang=en), [Content creator tips for beginners](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/content-creator-tips-for-beginners?lang=en)

### Inferences
- Official TikTok material supports a normal start: complete the profile, post original content, engage genuinely, and keep a cadence you can sustain. It does not prescribe browsing or engaging for days before the first post. "Warm-up before posting" routines are therefore community practice, not TikTok guidance.
- TikTok officially says the FYF learns from likes, shares, comments and searches. So what a new account watches and engages with plausibly shapes what it is shown. TikTok does not say that this affects how widely the account's own posts are distributed.
- For a personal creator who may want monetisation later, the official signals point to starting as a personal account rather than a business account.

### Gaps
- I could not open Creator Academy's "Get started" articles in full, including the "8 tips" article, so its specific tips are not captured.
- I found no official TikTok number for how often individual (non-publisher) creators should post, and no primary source for the "1–4 times a day" figure.
- I found no official guidance on when a new account should start posting, or how much it should engage before posting.
- TikTok for Business small-business guidance was not retrieved.

## 3. What activity limits exist or are widely reported (following cap, daily/hourly follow, like, comment and DM limits, "tapping too fast" and "too many attempts" blocks, and how long blocks last)? Official vs reported

### Takeaway
I found no official TikTok source that publishes numeric daily or hourly caps for follows, likes, comments or DMs. Officially, TikTok describes enforcement by behaviour pattern (spam, automation, repetition), temporary feature restrictions, verification challenges, and a strike system in which strikes expire after 90 days. A total following cap of 10,000 accounts is very consistently reported, and users report hitting it, but I could not confirm it on an official TikTok page. Marketer and user reports cluster around:
- **Follows:** about 200 per day, roughly 10–30 per hour.
- **Likes:** about 500 per day for established accounts, lower for new accounts.
- **Comments:** about 200 per day, roughly 20–30 per hour.
- **"You're tapping too fast" blocks:** minutes to about 24 hours, stretching to days with repetition.

All of these reported figures are anecdotal, inconsistent between sources, and come mostly from SEO or vendor blogs.
