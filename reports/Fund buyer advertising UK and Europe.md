# Reach fund gatekeepers where they actually decide

The strongest non-trade methods for a boutique UCITS manager are not media buys. Together they work as a distribution system. Build an auditable universe of a few hundred central buying desks and add clustered groups from the long tail of small adviser firms. Reach them with LinkedIn company-list clusters and search intent. Keep the fund's data spotless in the research platforms and AI agents where funds are chosen, and earn attention with owned assets that give buyers something of value, such as accredited CPD, bookable portfolio-manager office hours and proprietary indices. The reason is structural. **78.3% of 978 UK fund selectors** say they usually discover new funds on research platforms, against **4.7%** through contact from fund managers. UK decision power has concentrated in model-portfolio (MPS) teams, a market of roughly **£208–214bn growing 24–32% a year**, in the ten largest wealth firms (about **59% of discretionary assets**) and in consolidators, **84%** of which run their own model portfolios. Continental Europe has the same two-tier shape: central bank, insurer and network teams decide what tens of thousands of individual advisers are able to buy. Money is not the binding constraint, since about **$2,265 a month** buys four LinkedIn impressions a month for 10,000 targeted UK professionals. The real constraints are audience size, LinkedIn's **300-member minimum**, compliance capacity and each fund's registration status. Several fashionable tools fit badly. Enterprise account-based marketing suites cost an estimated **$118k–214k** in year one, and matching IP addresses to companies identifies only **5–10%** of small-firm staff working remotely. ChatGPT ads are a consumer channel whose paid business seats see no ads at all. The AI change that matters runs through data: Morningstar's records now reach Claude, ChatGPT and Copilot through connectors, so accurate fund data now works as advertising. Evidence on outcomes is thin. The research found no UK or European manager that has published attribution of fund flows to campaigns, and most benchmarks come from vendors, so the programme should be measured with randomised account holdouts from its first month.

## A few hundred desks decide what tens of thousands can buy

The UK looks like a long tail but behaves like an oligopoly. The FCA counts about **5,548 advice firms and 31,000 advisers**, with firm numbers down about 15% since 2021 ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/news-stories/fca-publishes-findings-financial-adviser-market-survey)), and 88% of firms are small ([FCA FOI](https://www.fca.org.uk/freedom-information/information-financial-advice-data-request-survey-july-2025)). Investment decisions, however, are moving to a much smaller set of central teams. About 500 firms manage investments, and the ten largest serve up to **89% of clients** and hold about **59% of discretionary assets** ([FCA wealth management survey 2026](https://www.fca.org.uk/data/wealth-management-survey-report-2026); [The Intermediary](https://theintermediary.co.uk/2026/08/89-of-wealth-management-clients-now-served-by-10-largest-firms-fca-finds/)). Model portfolio services held **£214bn at end-2025** by Platforum's count ([The Wealth Mosaic](https://www.thewealthmosaic.com/vendors/the-wealth-mosaic/twm-articles/event-summary-technology-scale-and-customisation-m/)) and £208bn after a 32% rise by NextWealth's ([IFA Magazine](https://ifamagazine.com/discretionary-mps-assets-reach-208bn-after-32-surge/)). Among the consolidators NextWealth profiled, **84% run in-house model portfolios and 68% have their own fund range** ([Evidence Investor](https://www.evidenceinvestor.com/post/financial-advice-consolidation-uk)). An acquisition can therefore shut third-party funds out of hundreds of advice firms at once. St. James's Place alone runs one centrally governed proposition for **4,951 advisers** ([SJP half-year report 2026](https://data.fca.org.uk/artefacts/NSM/RNS/89386544-c3d0-45cc-b3a3-f6932216c681.html)). Put together, these figures suggest that roughly 100–300 central buying units influence most UK intermediated fund flows. That is the research team's estimate, not a published figure, but it changes the brief: the job is to reach named committees, not a mass audience.

These desks find funds in data, not in inboxes. FE fundinfo's survey of 978 UK selectors found that 78.3% usually discover new funds through research platforms such as Trustnet, and only 4.7% through direct contact from managers ([FE fundinfo](https://www.fefundinfo.com/insights/where-uk-fund-selectors-really-find-new-ideas)). The source owns such platforms and so has an interest in that answer, but no other finding in the research points the other way. Advisers have also cut the number of discretionary managers they work with: from 2.5 on average in 2020 to 1.3 in 2024, rising to 1.7 in 2025, partly because Consumer Duty rewards consistency ([IFA Magazine](https://ifamagazine.com/discretionary-mps-assets-set-to-surpass-200bn-after-surging-a-third-in-a-year/)). The long tail of small firms mostly chooses which model portfolio service, discretionary manager and platform to use; it rarely picks single funds. Inside firms the researcher is often a paraplanner. **77% of medium-sized and 89% of large advice firms employ paraplanners directly, against 31% of small firms** ([Professional Paraplanner, reporting the FCA survey](https://professionalparaplanner.co.uk/development/what-the-fcas-2025-advice-market-survey-means-for-you/)), so audience profiles should be split by firm size. The data to act on this now exists. ISS Market Intelligence's MarketPro shows which adviser firms use each model-portfolio provider ([ISS MI](https://insights.issgovernance.com/posts/iss-market-intelligence-adds-new-features-to-marketpro)), and ISS's purchase of Autus added FCA-register coverage of **70,000+ firms and 220,000 individuals** ([ISS](https://insights.issgovernance.com/posts/iss-market-intelligence-acquires-autus-data-services-to-strengthen-u-k-intermediary-data-capabilities)). That makes a sharp creative split possible. Advisers whose model-portfolio providers already hold the fund get reinforcement; advisers whose providers do not get messages designed to build demand back towards those providers.

Continental Europe has larger registered populations but the same two-tier logic. Italy has **54,783 registered consulenti finanziari** ([Teleborsa](https://www.teleborsa.it/News/2026/07/07/consulenti-finanziari-cresce-albo-aumentano-aspiranti-in-particolare-i-giovani-157.html)), about 24,457 of them in the main Assoreti networks ([Advisoronline](https://advisoronline.it/consulenti-finanziari/numeri-delle-reti/reclutamenti-le-reti-che-si-stanno-irrobustendo-banca-mediolanum-accelera)), but each network decides centrally which third-party funds go on its shelf. In France, insurance generates **67% of CNCGP members' revenue** ([Haussmann-Fusac](https://haussmann-fusac.fr/ressources/barometre-cncgp-2026-les-chiffres-cles-dune-profession-en-pleine-croissance/)), so the fund lists insurers offer inside unit-linked policies control most CGP demand. Spain's independent channel is tiny, at **88 EAFs and 52 EAFNs advising €17.15bn** at end-2024 ([CNMV](https://www.cnmv.es/webservices/verdocumento/ver?e=ZBKbShY+9GuHqrBgZVA71KEpI+iFfPy6xtr5zzoQYHmkKR5xrcqgTLzGLccEARss)), which leaves bank selectors dominant. Germany's **41,388 §34f intermediaries include only about 375 fee-based §34h advisers** ([DIHK](https://www.dihk.de/de/serviceportal/fuer-gewerbetreibende-unternehmer/statistiken-und-bestandszahlen-zum-vermittlerregister-141724)), and the independent asset managers' association VuV has 358 member firms ([Bundestag Lobbyregister](https://www.lobbyregister.bundestag.de/suche/R005329)). The headline register therefore overstates the number of real fund selectors more than tenfold. Switzerland can be counted most precisely. FINMA had licensed 1,664 portfolio managers and trustees by end-2025, 97 of which have since left supervision ([FINMA](https://report.finma.ch/2025/en/market-developments/market-developments-in-the-asset-management-industry)). That leaves roughly 1,300–1,350 independent asset managers, and **56% of Swiss independent wealth managers are run by three people or fewer** ([ValIndex](https://valindex.ch/en/research/swiss-wealth-managers-2026/)). No census of professional fund selectors across Europe exists. Broadridge interviews more than 800 a year across ten countries ([Broadridge](https://www.broadridge.com/resource/market-analytics)), which points to a core population in the low thousands.

The audience is also mostly out of market. The Ehrenberg-Bass "95:5" rule of thumb holds that only about 5% of B2B buyers are buying in a given quarter ([Marketing Week](https://www.marketingweek.com/peter-weinberg-jon-lombardo-95-5-rule)). Fund buyers come into the market at predictable moments: quarterly model-portfolio rebalances, annual buy-list reviews, UK funds' annual Assessments of Value under COLL 6.6.20R ([FCA Handbook](https://www.handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/COLL/6/6.html)) and Consumer Duty fair-value reviews. Trigger events, such as a manager leaving a rival fund, add unscheduled ones. One channel fact also changes for German-speaking markets. Xing has been rebuilt as a jobs portal ([Lexware](https://office.lexware.de/blog/xing-recruiting/)), which leaves LinkedIn as the professional network of record across Germany, Austria and Switzerland. The map below turns these numbers into targeting units.

| Segment | Scale | Where the decision sits | Primary digital route |
|---|---|---|---|
| UK discretionary, model-portfolio and consolidator investment teams | ~500 investment firms; model portfolios £208–214bn | Investment committees, fund analysts | Named-account LinkedIn, PM access, due-diligence readiness, flow data |
| UK advice firms | ~5,548 firms, ~31,000 advisers | Mostly choose model-portfolio service, discretionary manager and platform; paraplanners research in larger firms | LinkedIn clusters by job title, search, risk-tool presence, CPD |
| Italy | 54,783 consulenti; ~24,500 in the big networks | Network product teams set the shelf; consulenti allocate within it | Account-based marketing (ABM) to shelf teams, plus Italian-language video to consulenti |
| France | ~7,000 CIFs; insurance 67% of CGP revenue | Insurers' and platforms' unit-linked fund lists | ABM to insurer selectors, French-language CPD |
| Spain | 88 EAFs + 52 EAFNs (€17bn) | Selectors at banks and bank-owned fund managers (gestoras) | ABM to banks; every EAF/EAFN treated as a named account |
| Germany | 41,388 §34f; ~375 §34h; 358 VuV firms | Banks, broker pools, independent asset managers (Vermögensverwalter) | LinkedIn (not Xing), pool events, email only with consent |
| Switzerland | ~1,300–1,350 licensed independent asset managers | Founders of small firms; private banks | Full named coverage from the FINMA list as one LinkedIn cluster |

## Paid precision means LinkedIn clusters, search intent and little else

LinkedIn remains the only paid channel that can pick out named fund-buying firms, and the roles inside them, at scale. By late 2026, though, it is less precise than its reputation. A campaign needs at least **300 matched members** to serve ([LinkedIn Marketing Solutions](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/ads/audience)). Uploaded company lists typically match 60–90% and contact lists 30–70%, and small or commonly named firms match worst ([ZenABM](https://zenabm.com/blog/linkedin-matched-audiences); [ZenABM](https://zenabm.com/blog/linkedin-contact-targeting)). Lookalike audiences were retired in early 2024. Their replacement, Predictive Audiences, needs a seed list of at least 300 ([DestinationCRM](https://www.destinationcrm.com/Articles/CRM-News/CRM-Featured-Articles/LinkedIn-Ends-Lookalike-Audiences-162839.aspx); [Edelman](https://www.edelman.com/insights/evolution-linkedin-audience-expansion-tools)). A single third-party source reports that LinkedIn folded its AI "Accelerate" campaign type into standard campaigns on 4 August 2026 and now switches Audience Expansion on by default wherever it is available ([Magnet.co](https://www.magnet.co/posts/audience-expansion-is-on-until-you-say-no)). Agencies also warn that the LinkedIn Audience Network buys "expensive clicks wasted on consumer browsing" for niche B2B offers ([Opteo](https://opteo.com/docs/en/articles/12269607-disable-audience-network-in-linkedin-ads)). For a promotion meant only for professionals, both settings should be off in every ad set, because expansion is exactly what turns a professional audience into a retail one.

The 300-member floor dictates the design. A five-person IFA practice cannot be targeted on its own; roughly sixty such firms have to be pooled into one ad set. The practical unit is a **cluster of similar firms**: UK model-portfolio teams, platform fund-list teams, Swiss independent asset managers, French CGP networks, Italian network product teams, German independent asset managers (Vermögensverwalter), Spanish EAFs, or private-bank selectors country by country. Each cluster is built from company lists drawn from the regulators' registers. Adding LinkedIn Page URLs and web domains lifts match rates ([B2Linked](https://b2linked.com/blog/best-match-rates-linkedin-ads/)), and job function and seniority filters sit on top. A cluster clears the minimum, carries one language and one approved set of ads, and keeps financial-promotion sign-off manageable. Large banks, insurers and platforms with more than 300 matched staff can be targeted on their own. LinkedIn's Buyer Group option, reportedly launched in February 2026, adds ready-made groups of likely decision-makers inside an account ([Optimize LinkedIn Ads](https://www.optimizelinkedinads.com/blogs/linkedin-buyer-group-targeting-ads-guide)), though its templates are built for generic purchases rather than investment committees. The Companies tab then scores each account's engagement across ads, organic posts and website visits ([ZenABM](https://zenabm.com/blog/see-companies-engaging-with-linkedin-ads)), giving sales a weekly view of which accounts are warming up without any personal data being uploaded.

People outperform logos in this market, and Thought Leader Ads are how to use that. A firm can sponsor a portfolio manager's own post to its clusters, or, with permission, a post by a client or an independent expert ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-will-now-enable-brands-to-sponsor-any-organic-post-in-the-app/710255/)). The format has hard limits. Document posts, polls and reshares cannot be sponsored. The only campaign objectives allowed are awareness, engagement and video views. The post runs as written, with no call-to-action button ([ZenABM](https://zenabm.com/blog/linkedin-thought-leader-ads-ultimate-guide); [JoinValley](https://www.joinvalley.co/blog/linkedin-thought-leader-ads)). A sequence that works within those limits has three steps: a portfolio manager's post or video opens the conversation; people who engage are retargeted with Document Ads and Lead Gen Forms that put the factsheet or strategy paper behind a short form; and, in the UK only, a Conversation Ad invites them to a meeting. The UK-only step matters. In the EEA and Switzerland, Sponsored Messaging reaches only members who have opted in, and advertisers cannot see how many have done so ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a427509); [DemandSense](https://demandsense.com/blog/linkedin-conversation-ads-vs-message-ads)). Targeting by LinkedIn Group membership has also been off for EU members since June 2024 under the Digital Services Act ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/linkedin-discontinues-group-based-ad-targeting-to-comply-with-dsa/)). The one documented European asset-manager result fits this pattern. Neuberger Berman's LinkedIn video campaign to Italian advisers retargeted people who had watched more than 30% of its films. It reported a **4.13% engagement rate** and a cost per lead **61% below benchmark** ([LinkedIn](https://business.linkedin.com/marketing-solutions/case-studies/neuberger-berman)), but the case is undated, published by LinkedIn itself, and does not define the benchmark.

The economics favour precision over scale. A 2026 dataset puts the median UK LinkedIn cost per thousand impressions (CPM) at **$56.62** ([ZenABM](https://zenabm.com/blog/linkedin-ads-benchmarks-2026)), and a Dutch benchmark puts Western Europe at **€73.83** ([Searchlab](https://searchlab.nl/statistieken/linkedin-ads-statistieken-2026)). At those rates, four impressions a month to 10,000 targeted members costs about $2,265 in the UK and €2,953 on the continent, so a three-month sequence costs less than $10,000 in media. Dreamdata's 2025 analysis of more than 220,000 B2B customer journeys found that LinkedIn returned 113% on ad spend, against 67% for Google Search and 51% for Meta, and that journeys averaged 211 days ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/linkedin-emerges-as-top-b2b-platform-with-113-roas-and-211-day-buyer-journeys/)). The trap is cheap traffic. Broad "finance and insurance" targeting produces costs per click (CPC) near $3.37 because it includes consumers, against $5–20 for enterprise finance roles ([Sales-Mind](https://sales-mind.ai/blog/2025-sales-funnel-benchmarks-linkedin); [Nav43](https://nav43.com/blog/linkedin-ads-benchmarks-for-fintech-the-definitive-guide-for-b2b-marketers-in-2025/)). Against a professional fund-buyer audience, an unusually low CPC or high click-through rate is a sign of leakage, not outperformance.

Search supplies the buying intent that LinkedIn lacks. In UK, French and German campaigns, Microsoft Advertising can target by LinkedIn company, industry and job function on search ads and on native "Audience ads" ([Microsoft Advertising](https://Advertising.Microsoft.com/en-us/solutions/Audience-targeting/LinkedIn-Profile-Targeting)). The seniority filter Microsoft added in June 2026 excludes exactly those three markets ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/microsoft-advertising-adds-job-seniority-to-linkedin-profile-targeting/)). Microsoft's rule is that "if an ad can serve on Microsoft Bing, it can also serve in Copilot", and financial-services ads keep their full terms there ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/advertising/msa-help/hlp_ba_conc_adsforcopilot)). A UK firm verified for financial-services advertising that bids on its own fund names, ISINs and strategy terms such as "global equity income fund" therefore appears inside Copilot answers automatically. That bidding is also needed to defend the brand, because rivals can advertise against the same queries.

On Google, the precise tools are search on fund names and ISINs, plus custom audiences built from those searches to target YouTube and Demand Gen ads. Uploading contact lists (Customer Match) reportedly needs 90 days of account history and $50,000 of lifetime spend for full use ([Storegrowers](https://www.storegrowers.com/google-customer-match/)), plus explicit consent signals for every upload covering EEA users ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/14310715)). Lookalike audiences need 1,000 matched people in the seed list ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/13541369)), which rules them out for most fund-buyer lists. Two recent changes need action. Google's financial-services advertiser verification became mandatory in 24 more EU/EEA markets from 23 July 2026, with 30 days to verify ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-expands-financial-services-ad-verification-across-24-european-markets-480833)). Google also automatically moved broad-match campaigns to AI Max in September 2026 ([Blackfire Marketing](https://blackfiremarketing.co.uk/blog/google-ai-mode-paid-search)), so its text customisation and final-URL expansion should be reviewed before unapproved copy runs in a regulated account. Bidding on competitors' fund names is lawful in the UK in principle, provided the ad implies no connection with the rival ([LexisNexis on Interflora v M&S](https://blogs.lexisnexis.co.uk/content/wipit/interflora-v-ms-adwords-are-ok-says-court-of-appeal)), though rules on fair comparison still apply.

Enterprise account-based marketing suites are the wrong default. Procurement data put median 6sense contracts at about $55,000–63,000 a year before any media spend ([Landbase](https://www.landbase.com/blog/6sense-pricing)). Demandbase costs $29,000–65,000, plus reported display minimums of $5,000–10,000 a month and about $29,000 for onboarding ([Docket](https://docket.io/resources/research/demandbase-pricing)). That implies a first year of roughly $118,000–214,000. These suites identify visiting companies from IP addresses, and that works worst where fund buyers sit. Vendors expect 50–65% matching for enterprise offices but **5–10% for remote small-business staff** ([MarketBetter](https://marketbetter.ai/blog/best-website-visitor-identification-tools-2026/)). No source confirms that Bombora, the main supplier of intent data, has fund-specific topics ([Bitscale](https://bitscale.ai/blogs/bombora-review)).

A leaner European stack does most of the job: LinkedIn's own account-based tools as the core, Dealfront/Leadfeeder alerts on which companies visit the site from about €79 a month ([Abmatic](https://blog.abmatic.ai/leadfeeder-pricing)), and optionally N.Rich for account-based display ads, with data hosted in the EU and consent collected through the IAB framework ([N.Rich](https://nrich.io/abm-platform-for-europe-and-emea)). Sales activity can drive the media too. Rolling CRM lists, such as contacts with a meeting in the next fortnight, recent factsheet downloaders and webinar attendees, can be synced to LinkedIn and pooled past 300 members. LinkedIn takes up to 24 hours to process a list ([Gartner support](https://support.ops.gartner.com/support/solutions/articles/70000640455-linkedin-faqs)), so contacts should be added a few days ahead. A buying group then sees the portfolio manager's latest post in the days before and after its meeting. Triggering at firm level, by adding a "hot" account to a company list, avoids uploading personal data altogether.

### Premium business media buy context, not precision

The best-documented premium environments run on data their readers supplied when registering. The **FT** claims job-function, industry and seniority data on more than 14 million registered users and sells direct or through private deals, having switched off open programmatic buying in 2018 ([FT advertiser deck 2026](https://www.guernseyfinance.com/media/k0wlvkdn/digital-at-the-ft-deck-2026.pdf); [WARC](https://www.warc.com/newsandopinion/opinion/understanding-the-fts-approach-to-data-collection-and-ad-targeting/4316)). **Bloomberg**'s Audience Accelerator models job title, industry and seniority across about 5 million registered users ([AdExchanger](https://adexchanger.com/publishers/bloomberg-opens-up-its-first-party-data-to-advertisers)). In **Germany**, iq digital's decision-maker segments across Handelsblatt, WirtschaftsWoche, FAZ, SZ and Zeit can be bought programmatically via emetriq ([emetriq](https://www.emetriq.com/?p=11018)). In **France**, Les Echos sells an "Investisseur" segment of about 2.5 million finance readers ([The Media Leader FR](https://fr.themedialeader.com/?p=87814)). In the **Netherlands**, the FD reaches 26,000 finance-industry readers a day, with a selectivity index of 409 among finance professionals ([FDMG](https://fdmg.nl/en/news/het-financieele-dagblad-vital-to-finance-professionals/)). In **Switzerland**, NZZ's The Market targets independent wealth managers and family offices ([Horizont Schweiz](https://www.horizont.net/schweiz/nachrichten/mark-dittli-chefredaktor-von-the-market-wir-greifen-relevante-und-spannende-themen-heraus-175081)), which makes it a boundary case under a strict no-trade rule.

Academic evidence favours a publisher's own reader data over third-party "B2B segments" bought on ad exchanges ([Neumann et al., 2023](https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/qmktec/v21y2023i4d10.1007_s11129-023-09268-7.html)). None of these publishers offers a "fund selector" segment, though. They suit brand and thought-leadership reach in a premium setting, with ads that are safe for retail readers to see, not conversion. Buying on attention rather than impressions is one option. The FT's "cost per hour" model, piloted by iShares among others, charged only for ads seen for more than five seconds of active use ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2015/05/19/financial-times-advertising)); whether it is still sold was not verified.

Video and audio follow the same rule. LinkedIn BrandLink runs ads before videos from Bloomberg, Reuters, the WSJ, The Economist, BBC Studios, BNR and Der Spiegel inside the LinkedIn feed, with LinkedIn's professional targeting. The UK and Germany are among its top markets ([LinkedIn News](https://news.linkedin.com/2025/helping-brands-expand-their-reach-in-new-ways-with-video-from-to); [Reuters via TradingView](https://de.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com%2C2025%3Anewsml_L4N3UD13D%3A0-linkedin-deepens-video-ad-push-taps-more-publishers-and-creators-to-spur-growth)), which makes it the strongest verified non-trade video option. LinkedIn's connected-TV (CTV) ads with job-title targeting are confirmed only for the US and Canada ([Mobile Marketing Magazine](https://mobilemarketingmagazine.com/linkedin-ctv-ads/)). European CTV now takes 53% of premium video ad views ([Señal News](https://senalnews.com/en/digital/ctv-accounts-for-53-of-europes-premium-video-ad-views)), but it targets households, which wastes most of the spend on an audience in the low tens of thousands.

Finance podcasts reach a diffuse audience: only 43% of Odd Lots listeners worked in finance in 2021 ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2021/05/heres-how-bloomberg-plans-to-turn-a-popular-podcast-into-paying-subscribers/)). Some of the most professional shows keep advertisers away from interview slots altogether ([Macro Voices](https://www.macrovoices.com/guest-pitches)). Cheap host-read sponsorships, and programmatic audio targeted by city through Spotify's Ad Exchange in the UK, Germany, France, Spain and Italy ([Spotify Ads](https://ads.spotify.com/en-GB/programmatic-advertising/spotify-ad-exchange)), therefore buy frequency, not precision. A sharper option is an owned podcast for professionals only, such as Schroders' *The Investor Download* ([Schroders](https://www.schroders.com/de-de/de/institutionelle/the-investor-download-podcast/)), promoted to the LinkedIn clusters.

## The fund's data record has become its most-viewed advertisement

### Screening tools are the real point of sale

If four in five selectors discover funds on research platforms, then a fund's record inside those platforms is the advertisement most of them actually see. In the UK the central tool is FE Analytics, which FE says is used by **84% of the top 100 UK advisers** and more than 3,900 adviser firms. FE also sells managers intelligence showing how advisers analyse and sort prospective funds, and which funds in a sector are bought and sold most ([FE fundinfo](https://www.fe-fundinfo.com/en-au/fund-managers/investment-research/fe-analytics/)). Its MPS directory lists more than 75 model-portfolio providers ([FE fundinfo](https://fefundinfo.com/insights/fe-fundinfo-s-mps-directory-reaches-over-75-providers-in-7-years)). Its purchase of Finscape adds flow intelligence from more than 15 retail platforms, which FE pitches as showing "who is actually making the buying decision behind that platform" ([FE fundinfo](https://www.fefundinfo.com/products/institutions/finscape)). That is a vendor claim, and whether a manager can see its own funds' flows firm by firm, rather than platform by platform, is the first question to ask in due diligence.

Adviser risk-profiling software is the most literal point of work. Dynamic Planner risk-profiles funds and model portfolios for more than 150 asset managers, covering more than 1,700 funds ([Dynamic Planner](https://dynamicplanner.com/asset-managers/)). Advisers then find those funds inside the suitability workflow, filtered by client risk level. Schroders went furthest, launching five co-branded portfolios built to Dynamic Planner risk levels 3 to 7 ([Dynamic Planner](https://dynamicplanner.com/blog/dynamic-planner-schroders-launch-5-cobranded-risk-targeted-funds/)). For a boutique, the realistic version is to pay for profiling only the funds that fit advised portfolios, such as income, multi-asset and global equity, and to check that they appear in advisers' filters.

The same ecosystem carries a conflict-of-interest risk. Fintel, which owns Defaqto, the SimplyBiz adviser-membership business and, since January 2025, the fund research firm RSMR, openly sells "data, distribution, and marketing and promotion services to product providers" ([Fintel results](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/fintel--fntl/full-year-results/7479811); [LSE RNS](https://www.lse.co.uk/rns/FNTL/half-year-trading-update-0w7qtb9n24vl0vd.html)). Ratings and paid promotion under one roof invite a pay-to-play perception that compliance should assess before any spend. UK platform rules set a similar boundary. Since PS13/1, fund managers may pay platforms only for limited services, such as advertising on the platform or management information, and the payments must not vary inappropriately ([FCA PS13/1](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/ps13-1-payments-platform-service-providers-and-cash-rebates-providers); [Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/guides/-platforms-and-remuneration)). Paying for a place on a fund list is the preferential treatment the rules were written to stop.

### Continental fund platforms sell intelligence, not banners

On the continent, distribution runs through business-to-business fund platforms, and they sell fund houses data rather than advertising space. Allfunds offers access to more than 850 distributors under one contract ([Allfunds](https://allfunds.com/en/fund-houses)) and reached a record **€1.76tn of assets under administration** in 2025, adding 64 distributors ([Allfunds FY2025 results](https://app.allfunds.com/docs/cms/Press_Release_FY_25_Preliminary_Results_VF_03eb1ecb7a.pdf)). Its main data products are Telemetrics 2.0, which breaks down flows and assets by distributor client and region ([Hubbis](https://hubbis.com/news/allfunds-new-tools-give-major-boost-to-fund-managers)), and Connect widgets, which show the most-visited and most-compared funds based on browsing by professional investors at more than 800 institutions ([Allfunds](https://allfunds.com/blog/2024/07/01/Allfunds_Connect_widgets)). Euroclear finished absorbing MFEX on 1 January 2026 and now connects 250,000 funds with more than 3,000 distributors and 2,500 asset managers ([Euroclear](https://www.euroclear.com/newsandinsights/en/press/2026/mr-01-euroclear-successfully-integrates-mfex.html)). Clearstream Fund Centre serves more than 450 distributors and runs "Compass", a shared fund-information platform linking fund providers and distributors ([Clearstream](https://clearstream.com/clearstream-en/funds-services/fund-centre)). The research found no banner packages or rate cards on any of these platforms.

For a UK boutique with no local sales teams, three things matter in this channel. Onboarding comes first, because a fund that is not on the platform cannot be found by the bank and private-bank selectors who use it. Distributor-flow data is the most valuable purchase, because it shows which distributors already buy rival funds in the same category, so a small sales team can aim its effort. Content published inside distributor portals is the nearest thing to advertising available. Buying data also carries less inducement risk than paying distributors for visibility.

Timing matters too. Deutsche Börse agreed to buy Allfunds for about €5.3bn, shareholders approved the deal in March 2026, and completion is expected in the first half of 2027 ([Deutsche Börse](https://deutsche-boerse.com/dbg-en/media/news-stories/press-releases/Deutsche-B-rse-Group-s-Recommended-Acquisition-of-Allfunds-Shareholder-Approvals-of-Allfunds-Obtained-5008700)). One group will then own both Allfunds and Clearstream Fund Centre, so data contracts should be timed for likely repackaging and repricing in 2027.

Germany's fund platforms that serve advisers are service providers rather than media owners, and visible fund-house marketing happens at broker-pool events instead. Fonds Finanz's MMM in Munich drew about 150 exhibitors and more than 100 talks, many of them certified for advisers' compulsory insurance-distribution (IDD) training hours ([Fonds Finanz](https://www.fondsfinanz.de/ff/dokumente/presse/2026/pressemitteilung-mmm-2026-ankuendigung.pdf)). Across Europe, complete FinDatEx data templates (EMT for target market, EPT for PRIIPs cost data, EET for ESG data) are a precondition for being selectable on many platforms and buy lists ([FinDatEx](https://findatex.eu/)).

### AI agents now read the same records

Advisers' use of AI has risen fast, but mostly for admin. Intelliflo found that 74% of UK advice professionals use AI, up from 43% a year earlier, mainly to cut admin work ([Money Marketing](https://www.moneymarketing.co.uk/news/ai-adoption-among-advisers-jumps-to-74-intelliflo-finds/)). FE fundinfo's survey found that 95% of advice firms have "deployed AI tooling" ([FE fundinfo](https://www.fefundinfo.com/insights/financial-adviser-survey-2026-five-takeaways-for-every-advice-firm)). Dynamic Planner puts active use at 41%, up from 28% ([Dynamic Planner](https://dynamicplanner.com/resources/advice-report-voice-of-the-financial-advice-industry/)). In wealth firms, the FCA counts only about 13% using AI today ([FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/data/wealth-management-survey-report-2026)). None of these surveys supports the claim that advisers pick funds with ChatGPT, and no survey found measures generative-AI use in fund selection by professional gatekeepers. That remains an evidence gap.

What has changed is the plumbing beneath the tools selectors already pay for. **Morningstar Direct AI**, launched in October 2026, runs separate agents for manager research and for distribution ([FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/morningstar-direct-ai-turns-fund-research-into-three-agent-workflows/)). **Morningstar's MCP server**, an official read-only connector for licensed users, works with Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot Studio, Microsoft Foundry and Perplexity ([Morningstar Developer](https://developer.morningstar.com/direct-web-services/documentation/morningstar-mcp-server/about)); MCP (Model Context Protocol) is the standard that lets AI assistants call outside data sources. **Copilot in Excel** added connectors to LSEG and Moody's data in May 2026, then to FactSet, Morningstar and S&P Global in June ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/microsofts-copilot-in-excel-adds-skills-and-data-connectors-for-finance-work-93CH-4761214)). **Anthropic's "Claude for Financial Advisors"** includes connectors from two asset managers, BlackRock and Vanguard ([Claude](https://claude.com/blog/claude-for-financial-advisors); [BlackRock Advisor Center connector](https://claude.com/marketplace/connectors/blackrock-advisor-center)).

One Morningstar record now feeds half a dozen AI front ends. Any mismatch between a vendor record and the manager's own site, whether on charges, launch date, benchmark, manager names or a fund's old name after a rename, becomes an error that AI models repeat. Reconciling vendor records every month is now a marketing task.

A manager can also put its data into these tools directly. On 27 October 2026 the research team queried the public MCP server of one UK boutique, Guinness Global Investors, through a connection available in its research environment, and a repeat check while this report was written gave the same result. No public documentation was found. The server's own instructions limit the public endpoint to funds published on the firm's website and to prices the website already shows, which is the right default. A request for cumulative performance returned month-end figures at 30 September 2026 for all 15 share classes of its Asian Equity Income fund, two benchmarks and IA-sector quartiles and ranks. It returned **no risk warning or past-performance disclaimer**, and one configured time period was marked hidden.

That is a useful prototype and a warning at once. Restricting a public server to published figures is the right start, but every response should also carry, as structured fields, the as-of date, currency, ISIN, data source, standard warnings and links to the key information document (KID or KIID) and factsheet. Tool descriptions can also tell the AI model how to present the data, for example "always show discrete 12-month returns and the benchmark".

A second step could be a due-diligence connector for professionals only. It would hold questionnaire answers, team and process descriptions and lagged holdings behind a login checked against the FCA register or a firm's legal entity identifier (LEI). It would cut the time selectors spend chasing due-diligence answers, and its usage logs would become first-party intent data at firm level. The research found no UK or European asset manager or adviser-tech connector in the Claude or ChatGPT directories. That suggests, but does not prove, a first-mover window.

### Generative engine optimisation rewards dull, crawlable facts

Generative engine optimisation (GEO), the AI-era counterpart of search optimisation, works differently for funds than for most products. Answer engines build fund answers from search indexes, licensed data vendors and heavily cited third-party sites. Only 6.82% of ChatGPT results overlap with Google's top ten ([The Digital Bloom](https://thedigitalbloom.com/learn/ai-citation-position-revenue-report-2026/)), so ranking well on Google does not guarantee citation. Finance relies unusually heavily on brands' own pages: Yext found that **88% of finance citations come from brand-managed sources** ([AI Growth Agent](https://aigrowthagent.co/articles/ai-search-visibility-financial-brands/)). Yet OtterlyAI found that **73% of sites have technical barriers that block AI crawlers** ([Otterly.AI](https://otterly.ai/blog/the-ai-citations-report-2026/)). For fund managers, the likely culprits are investor-type click-through gates, cookie walls and bot protection, which can quietly remove a fund from AI answers.

OpenAI separates its search crawler (OAI-SearchBot) from its training crawler (GPTBot), so a site can allow one and block the other ([OpenAI](https://platform.openai.com/docs/bots)). Google says AI Overviews need only normal Search eligibility ([Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)). Visible FAQ sections raise the chance of being cited, but FAQ schema markup alone has no measurable effect ([Onely](https://www.onely.com/blog/how-investment-companies-can-get-cited-in-ai-answers/)). A study of about 300,000 domains found no significant link between having an llms.txt file and being cited ([Limy](https://limy.ai/blog/llms-txt-in-2026-the-full-guide)).

The playbook is unglamorous. Each share class needs one canonical HTML page keyed by ISIN, carrying its identifiers, sector, ongoing charge, benchmark, managers with their start dates and a dated price. Performance belongs in HTML tables with discrete 12-month periods and as-of dates, not only in PDFs, and schema.org's `InvestmentFund` markup ([schema.org](https://schema.org/InvestmentFund)) helps mainly to tell similar funds apart. Publishing should run in two tiers: a public, retail-safe factual layer that search crawlers can reach, and commentary for professionals behind the gate. A disambiguation page covering renamed funds and similarly named rivals removes a common source of AI errors. Off-site, portfolio-manager explainer videos with accurate transcripts matter because vendor analyses rank YouTube, Reddit and Wikipedia among the most-cited domains, and Reuters and the FT among the most-cited publishers ([Contently](https://contently.com/2026/04/29/top-sources-llms-cite/); [AuthorityTech](https://authoritytech.io/curated/which-publications-get-cited-ai-search-engines-2026)).

Accuracy is the reason this matters. One study found ChatGPT-4o hallucinated finance-literature references about 20% of the time ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.07031)), and published figures give models something to retrieve instead of something to invent. Measuring the result takes a fixed panel of selector-style prompts in English, German, French, Italian and Spanish, run through monitoring tools such as Profound or Peec AI ([Pepper](https://www.pepper.inc/blog/ai-visibility-tools-for-fintech/)). The panel should also be run inside enterprise tools connected to Morningstar, since those are what professional buyers actually use.

### AI ad inventory reaches consumers, not committees

The new AI advertising channels sit almost entirely outside the professional buyer's working day. ChatGPT ads went live in the UK on 6 June 2026 ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/chatgpt-ads-go-live-in-the-uk-as-openai-expands-pilot-beyond-us/)) and became self-serve in 31 European markets in late August ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-ads-expands-across-europe/)). They are shown only to Free and Go users; **Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu accounts see no ads** ([OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001047-ads-in-chatgpt)). OpenAI's own documents conflict on financial services. Its policy page says financial ads outside the US are "generally prohibited" ([OpenAI ad policies](https://openai.com/policies/ad-policies/)), while its help centre lists investing as "restricted to select approved advertisers" ([OpenAI Help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001534-troubleshooting-common-onboarding-and-policy-issues)).

Google shows ads inside AI Overviews in only 12 English-language markets, and the UK and the EU are not among them ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16297775?hl=en)). Perplexity stopped taking new advertisers in October 2025 ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/perplexity-stops-testing-advertising-469452)). The table summarises the position.

| AI channel (Oct 2026) | UK | EU/EEA/Switzerland | Fit for professional fund buyers |
|---|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT ads | Live since June 2026 | Self-serve since late Aug 2026 | Poor: consumer tiers only; investing ads need approval; any fund ad is a retail promotion |

## Education, tools and PM access earn the attention ads cannot buy

### Accredited education turns CPD hours into an owned audience

The best-documented non-trade success in this market is an education platform, not an ad campaign. Candriam Academy, a free online course in sustainable investing launched in 2017, grew to **16,400 members in 116 countries by 2024** ([Candriam CSR report 2025](https://csr.candriam.com/pdf/Candriam_2025_CSR%20Report-pages-Citizens.pdf)). It has been accredited for continuing-professional-development (CPD) credits, reportedly by EFPA France among others ([Candriam](https://institute.candriam.com/news20220307-candriam-academy-marks-the-10000-users-landmark)). No flow figures were published, but the manager built an audience it owns, in a market where most audiences are rented.

The demand is created by regulation. UK retail investment advisers must complete 35 hours of CPD a year, 21 of them structured ([FCA Handbook, TC](https://www.handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/TC/)). The EU's Insurance Distribution Directive requires 15 hours of training a year from insurance distributors, which covers the many French and Italian advisers who also sell unit-linked insurance ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2016/97/oj)). MiFID II counts training on a specific instrument's features as an acceptable minor non-monetary benefit ([Delegated Directive 2017/593](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir_del/2017/593/oj)). Pharma supplies the design rule. US standards for accredited medical education keep commercial sponsors away from the content ([ACCME](https://accme.org/)). A fund manager's academy should likewise keep the fund out of the modules, use independent faculty, and offer product follow-up separately and only on request.

Topics adjacent to the strategy work best: analysing dividend sustainability, designing income for clients drawing down their savings, or evidencing fair value under Consumer Duty. Distribution can run through LinkedIn Document Ads and Lead Gen Forms and through professional bodies' own events, which are associations rather than trade media. Spaced-repetition question sequences, which randomised trials in medical education found improve retention ([Qstream](https://qstream.com/)), make the format stick. A firm-against-firm leaderboard pays out only in charity donations, because personal prizes risk being inducements.

A verified-professional hub gives all this a durable base. In Germany, the law on medicines advertising (HWG) restricts prescription-drug promotion to healthcare professionals, so pharma companies put professional content behind DocCheck logins ([HWG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/heilmwerbg/); [DocCheck](https://www.doccheck.com/)). The fund equivalent is a sign-up that checks the firm's reference number against the [FCA Register](https://register.fca.org.uk/) or the ORIAS, OCF, CNMV and FINMA lists. Paraplanners, who are often not individually registered, can be verified by their firm's reference number plus a work email domain. The result is three assets in one: a verified first-party audience to match on LinkedIn, an opted-in email list, and a defensible "professional investors only" gate. The email list matters most in Germany, where unfair-competition law (UWG §7) requires prior consent even for business-to-business email ([UWG §7](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/uwg_2004/__7.html)).

The same hub can host the most underused asset a boutique has: access to its portfolio managers. A Thought Leader Ad from the PM can offer capped 20-minute office-hours slots for fund selectors, booked through a scheduling tool such as Calendly or Chili Piper ([Calendly](https://calendly.com/); [Chili Piper](https://www.chilipiper.com/)) after a firm check. Scarcity adds pull, as in luxury marketing, and mega-managers cannot easily copy it. No documented asset-manager example was found, so this is a test to run. Each session must be treated as a promotion: only invite people in countries where the fund is registered, keep records, and never discuss non-public portfolio changes.

### Pharma's approved-email machinery transfers almost intact

Pharma solved the problem of personalised but compliant follow-up years ago. With Veeva's Approved Email, sales reps can send only emails assembled from templates and content fragments already approved by medical, legal and regulatory reviewers. Sends and opens are logged against the doctor's record, and Veeva's closed-loop tool records which presentation slides each doctor viewed ([Veeva](https://www.veeva.com/)). Breaking content into pre-approved "modules" lets companies combine claims into many personalised assets without reviewing every combination ([Veeva Vault PromoMats](https://www.veeva.com/products/vault-promomats/)).

The fund version is a library of approved modules (fund one-liners, risk warnings, monthly-refreshed charts, PM quotes, CPD invitations) held in Seismic, Salesforce or HubSpot. Within 24 hours of a meeting, the salesperson assembles a follow-up from those modules and sends it from their own address, while the firm's buying group is retargeted on LinkedIn for several weeks. Large managers already have the tracking layer. LGIM uses Seismic LiveSend to see which sections a client read and for how long, and saves more than 10,000 hours a year on searching for and personalising content ([Seismic](https://seismic.com/customer-stories/lgim/)). Allianz Global Investors replaced static PDFs with Turtl's interactive documents so that it can measure how they are read ([casestudies.com](https://www.casestudies.com/company/turtl)).

Pharma's "market access" discipline offers a second lesson. US drug makers win formulary listings, the approved-medicines lists that payers maintain, with standard evidence dossiers such as the AMCP format, run separately from promotion to prescribers ([AMCP](https://www.amcp.org/)). The fund equivalent is a "buy-list access kit": a pre-filled due-diligence questionnaire (DDQ), complete EMT, EPT and EET data files, fair-value information, capacity and team-stability data, a named contact for research houses and platform fund-list teams, and a public promise such as any DDQ answered within five working days. It addresses what buyers say they want. US gatekeepers treat investment-team stability as a proxy for repeatable performance ([Finviz, on Broadridge's Fund Brand 50](https://finviz.com/news/341051/blackrock-retains-top-spot-in-the-us-2026-broadridge-fund-brand-50-report)). UK advisers' main frustrations when researching funds are jargon, vague statements of what a fund aims to do, and detail on how it is run that is hard to find ([Investment Association](https://www.theia.org/sites/default/files/2025-02/SDR%20investor%20and%20adviser%20awareness,%20understanding%20and%20expectations%202025_0.pdf)).

Consumer Duty adds a reason to help gatekeepers look good. It requires manufacturers to support intermediaries in helping retail customers understand products ([FCA Consumer Duty](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/consumer-duty)). Comprehension-tested explainers and summary packs ready for an investment committee are therefore both service and marketing. Versions meant for end clients, however, are retail promotions and must be kept strictly separate.

### Proprietary data assets give salespeople a reason to call

Professional-services firms have shown how one recurring research asset can serve as PR, advertising and sales tool at once. The Knight Frank Wealth Report combines a survey of private bankers, wealth advisers and family offices with proprietary indices ([Knight Frank](https://www.knightfrank.com/wealthreport)), so the intermediaries are both its sources and its audience. BlackRock's interactive Geopolitical Risk Dashboard gives professionals a standing reason to come back ([BlackRock](https://www.blackrock.com/corporate/insights/blackrock-investment-institute/interactive-charts/geopolitical-risk-dashboard)). Howard Marks's memos, published since 1990, show a named PM letter becoming a firm's main brand asset ([Oaktree](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memos)).

A boutique should go narrower and more frequent rather than imitate the banks' annual reports. One option is a quarterly index in its specialism, clearly different from Janus Henderson's established Global Dividend Index ([Janus Henderson](https://www.janushenderson.com/)). Another is a three-to-five-question "fund selector pulse" each quarter; Natixis's long-running Global Survey of Fund Selectors shows that selectors can be the subject of a manager's research ([Natixis IM](https://www.im.natixis.com/)). A third is a free tool, such as an energy-transition tracker built on Ember's openly licensed electricity data ([Ember](https://ember-energy.org/)), with an "export this chart for your investment committee" button.

Each asset carries known risks. Market-data redistribution licences can be costly. A tool that offers views on individual stocks can count as an investment recommendation under the Market Abuse Regulation. Any mention of the fund turns the asset into a promotion. The PM letter belongs on LinkedIn as a newsletter, whose subscribers are notified in the app and by email with each issue ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin)). The best issues can then be boosted as Thought Leader Ads to the account clusters. Readers should also be invited onto the firm's own list, because the audience follows the person, not the logo.

### Triggered and synthetic creative must run on pre-approved rails

Market events are when advisers' clients call and selectors recheck exposures, so creative that switches on with events earns attention. The working model is a library of three to five pre-approved variants per trigger. Triggers might be a sharp weekly move in Brent crude, a large move in gilt or Bund yields, a sector falling 10% from its peak, or a major dividend cut. Each variant carries PM commentary and is switched on within 24 hours, manually or through LinkedIn's Marketing API ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/)). The trigger may change which approved message runs, never the claims inside it. Live performance figures stay out, because past-performance rules require complete standard periods and warnings ([FCA COBS 4.6](https://www.handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/COBS/4/6.html)), and showing performance only when it looks good would be cherry-picking.

The most valuable trigger may be a competitor's manager leaving a fund in the same sector, which forces every selector holding that fund to review it. The response is a due-diligence pack ready on the shelf, an alert to sales and a short burst to the sector's buyer universe, without ever naming or disparaging the rival.

Personalisation and physical media extend the same logic. Spotify Wrapped, launched in 2016, set the template for personalised year-in-review content ([Spotify Newsroom](https://newsroom.spotify.com/)). A December digest for each engaged client firm can list the meetings held, the CPD hours completed and "the questions your team asked that shaped our research", with no performance numbers. "Information gifts", such as a printed annual, a data poster or the PM's book, can be triggered from the CRM through tools such as Reachdesk or Sendoso ([Reachdesk](https://reachdesk.com/)). A QR code leads to the firm's own landing page, the buying group is retargeted on LinkedIn, and a salesperson calls within a week. Under UK rules, post to named business contacts can rely on legitimate interests with an opt-out ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)). Gifts must still pass inducement tests, the Bribery Act ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/23/contents)) and the recipients' own gift policies.

For an analytical audience in a quiet December, a year-end puzzle offers distinctiveness at low compliance risk. Jane Street's monthly puzzle with its public list of solvers ([Jane Street](https://www.janestreet.com/puzzles/)) and GCHQ's Christmas puzzles ([GCHQ](https://www.gchq.gov.uk/)) show the format, provided prizes are charity donations and no product claims appear.

AI-dubbed portfolio-manager video is the most promising synthetic format for continental selectors. The real PM records in English, and AI produces German, French, Italian and Spanish versions, each checked by a native-speaking investment professional, because terms such as accumulating and distributing share classes must be exactly right. From 2 August 2026, Article 50(4) of the EU AI Act requires anyone using AI to generate or alter image, audio or video content that looks deceptively real (a "deep fake") to disclose that it is artificial ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)). The EU's 2026 package of amendments to the Act (the "Digital Omnibus") left that date unchanged ([Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/)). A visible label such as "AI-generated translation of [PM]'s English recording of [date]" is therefore required, together with the PM's written, revocable licence of their likeness and voice. Germany's art copyright law (KUG §22) independently requires a person's consent to disseminate their image ([KUG §22](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/kunsturhg/__22.html)). No asset-manager example could be verified, but local-language video also feeds local-language AI answers, which compounds the payoff.

People-based amplification needs the same rails. The FCA's FG24/1 guidance treats staff posts about their firm's products on personal accounts, and paid influencer content, as potential financial promotions ([FCA FG24/1](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/finalised-guidance/fg24-1-finalised-guidance-financial-promotions-social-media)). Sponsored Thought Leader posts cannot be edited, so only pre-approved posts should be boosted. Paying independent experts who do not advise clients, such as economists, energy analysts or technical trainers, works with disclosure. Paying practising advisers or fund selectors to promote a fund does not, because it is an inducement.

### Build memory before the review window opens

The 95:5 rule makes most of this programme a memory-building exercise. B2B effectiveness research puts the best long-run split at about **46% brand-building to 54% activation** ([LinkedIn B2B Institute](https://business.linkedin.com/marketing-solutions/b2b-institute)). Buyers spend only about 17% of their buying time with suppliers ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/sales/insights/b2b-buying-journey)). Personal value, meaning career safety and confidence, weighed about twice as much as business value in B2B purchase intent in CEB and Google's research ([Think with Google](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/)). Ehrenberg-Bass's work adds two tools ([Ehrenberg-Bass Institute](https://marketingscience.info/)): **category entry points**, the buying situations that cue a brand in a buyer's mind, and **distinctive brand assets**, recognisable cues that belong to one brand.

For fund buyers, plausible entry points include "when I need more income without adding duration", "when an income fund on our list cuts its dividend or loses its manager", "when clients ask about energy prices" and "Assessment of Value season". These are hypotheses to test in 15–20 buyer interviews. A boutique should own two or three of them and repeat the same phrasing in ads, factsheets, decks and the PM letter.

Fund marketing is visually uniform, which makes distinctiveness cheap: one colour, one chart style and one named recurring format, kept for years, with humour aimed at industry quirks and never at risk or returns. The practical rhythm is always-on, low-frequency presence across the whole account universe, plus bursts at quarter-ends, in Assessment of Value months, in each account's buy-list review month and around trigger events.

## Compliance architecture and account holdouts decide what scales

### "Professional investors only" is a claim that must be evidenced

Because the manager is FCA-authorised, its own promotions meet the section 21 restriction automatically ([FSMA s21](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2000/8/section/21)). What binds a B2B campaign is the conduct rulebook. Promotions must be fair, clear and not misleading. MiFID standards that reach professional clients too require risks to be shown prominently whenever benefits are mentioned ([COBS 4.5A](https://www.handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/COBS/4/5A.html)). Any past performance shown needs five complete 12-month periods, a stated source, a warning and, where relevant, a currency warning ([COBS 4.6](https://www.handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/COBS/4/6.html)). The FCA's social-media guidance adds that every post must be compliant on its own, without relying on a link or a "see more" ([FCA FG24/1](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/finalised-guidance/fg24-1-finalised-guidance-financial-promotions-social-media)).

The financial-promotion exemption for investment professionals treats a communication as aimed only at professionals if it meets three conditions. It must say it is for professionals, warn others not to rely on it, and have systems that stop other people from investing through the communicator ([FPO art 19](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2005/1529/article/19)). That third condition is hard to meet for UK OEICs that retail investors can buy on platforms. The manager then has to show that the promotion "may reasonably be regarded as directed only at" professionals, and LinkedIn job titles, being self-declared, count as supporting evidence rather than a safe harbour.

The workable answer is an evidence pack kept for every campaign. It holds account lists sourced from the regulators' registers, the function and seniority filters, exclusions of students, retirees, own staff and competitors, and confirmation that audience expansion and off-platform networks were switched off. The professional-only and capital-at-risk statements sit inside the creative itself. The landing page records each visitor's country and investor type. Delivery reports and periodic tests for leakage to retail audiences complete the pack.

Two further rules reach B2B creative. The Consumer Duty does not cover dealings with professional clients, but a manufacturer of retail funds is within it for its role in the distribution chain ([PRIN 2A](https://www.handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/PRIN/2A/)), so creative should be written on the assumption that it will be forwarded to retail clients. The FCA's anti-greenwashing rule applies to sustainability claims made to professionals as well ([ESG 4.3](https://www.handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/ESG/4/3.html)).

### A registration matrix is the real targeting gate

Across Europe, the binding question is not who the audience is but where the fund may be marketed. For Irish and Luxembourg UCITS, the EU management company (ManCo) is responsible for every marketing communication, including social posts and banners. Each one must be identifiable as marketing and give risks and rewards equal prominence ([Regulation 2019/1156](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2019/1156/oj)), and must carry ESMA's prescribed disclaimer ([ESMA guidelines](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/library/esma34-45-1272_guidelines_on_marketing_communications.pdf)). ESMA reads reverse solicitation narrowly. Internet advertising counts as solicitation, and pop-ups asserting that the investor took the initiative do not prove it ([ESMA35-43-2509](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/library/esma35-43-2509_statement_on_reverse_solicitation.pdf)). Geo-targeted ads into a country where a share class is not notified are therefore unlawful marketing. UK OEICs count as non-EU alternative investment funds in the EU, so they can be marketed only to professionals under each country's private-placement regime ([AIFMD](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2011/61/oj)). Switzerland is more permissive. Advertising must be identifiable and refer to the prospectus and key information document ([FinSA](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2019/758/en)), but offering a foreign fund only to qualified investors needs no FINMA approval ([CISA](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2006/822/en)).

The practical tool is a matrix of fund, share class, country, investor type and advertising entity. It feeds both the platforms' location targeting and the landing-page gates. It also has to handle segments whose status is uncertain. German §34f intermediaries hold trade licences, are often sole traders, and their status as per se professionals is uncertain, so they should be treated as retail for UK OEICs unless German counsel advises otherwise. Single family offices are often not per se professional either. UK sole-trader IFAs count as investment professionals for the promotion exemption, but as "individual subscribers" under the UK's e-privacy rules (PECR), so they need consent for email ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/business-to-business-marketing/)).

### Data and inducement law rank the tactics

Data law sorts the tactics clearly. Targeting on the platforms' own professional data is the safest precision route, because uploaded company names are not personal data. Retargeting through tracking pixels needs consent across the UK and EU. The CJEU's Planet49 judgment rejected pre-ticked consent boxes ([CJEU C-673/17](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62017CJ0673)), and the EU data-protection board's guidance brings pixels and IP-based tracking within the consent rule ([EDPB Guidelines 2/2023](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/guidelines/guidelines-22023-technical-scope-art-53-eprivacy-directive_en)). IP addresses can be personal data ([CJEU Breyer](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62014CJ0582)), so identifying a sole-trader adviser's firm from a website visit identifies a person. The exemption for analytics cookies in the UK's Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 does not extend to advertising ([DUAA 2025](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2025/18/contents)).

Email rules split by country. France allows email to professionals without consent where it concerns their profession and they can object ([CNIL](https://www.cnil.fr/fr/la-prospection-commerciale-par-courrier-electronique)). Germany requires prior consent. Switzerland treats unsolicited mass email as unfair ([Swiss UWG](https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1988/223_223_223/en)). German competitors may also sue over breaches of the GDPR ([CJEU C-21/23](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:62023CJ0021)). Platforms' own legal footing is fragile too: Ireland's Data Protection Commission fined LinkedIn €310m in 2024 over its legal basis for targeted ads ([DPC](https://www.dataprotection.ie/en/news-media/press-releases/irish-data-protection-commission-fines-linkedin-ireland-eu310-million)).

Inducement rules draw the other bright line. Firms giving independent advice or managing portfolios may accept only minor non-monetary benefits ([MiFID II](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2014/65/oj)). Paying creators who practise as advisers, or paying for prominence on a fund list, is therefore off-limits. Buying data at flat, market-rate prices is generally workable. ### Account holdouts, not click-through rates, prove impact

No UK or European manager in the research had published a validated link from digital exposure to account-level flows, and documented cases report engagement and efficiency rather than assets. The building blocks for a better answer now exist: platform flow intelligence from Finscape inside FE fundinfo's Nexus ([FE fundinfo](https://www.fefundinfo.com/insights/fe-fundinfo-unveils-unmatched-fund-flow-intelligence-with-acquisition-of-finscape)), the ISS MI/Autus account universe, Allfunds' data on professional browsing, and Broadridge's Fund Buyer Focus, which draws on more than 1,000 fund-buyer interviews a year ([Broadridge](https://www.broadridge.com/de/press-release/2018/broadridge-acquires-mackaywilliams)).

Academic work shapes the design. Gallaher, Kaniel and Starks found that the effect of advertising on fund-family flows is convex, appearing only for relatively heavy advertisers, and that at individual-fund level advertising mainly stems redemptions rather than adding purchases ([CEPR](https://cepr.org/publications/DP10329)). Two consequences follow. Spend should be concentrated on one or two flagship strategies rather than spread across the range. Measurement should track whether existing holders stay, not just gross inflows, and should control for fund performance.

The design that fits a boutique is a randomised holdout at account level. Define 300–1,500 target accounts and tier them by flow potential. Within each tier, randomly exclude 20–30% from paid media using LinkedIn company-list exclusions, keeping sales coverage constant or logging it. Each month, read leading indicators: engaged minutes on tracked content, meeting acceptance, fund-page visits on Allfunds, and the fund's share of branded fund-name and ISIN searches. Les Binet's IPA work found share of search predicted car brands' market share 9–12 months ahead ([IPA](https://ipa.co.uk/news/binet-presents-fast-cheap-predictive-share-of-search-metric/)). Log mid-funnel milestones, such as due-diligence requests and buy-list or model-portfolio inclusions, as CRM opportunities. Finally, read net flows per account over 6–18 months, comparing exposed and held-out accounts before and after, a difference-in-differences test.

Statistical power is the honest constraint. A few hundred concentrated accounts will rarely produce a significant flow result in one cycle, so leading indicators should guide decisions in flight. Flow results then serve as confirmation, built up over repeated cycles and stratified by account size.

Three failure modes are well documented. The first is vanity metrics: Cerulli found that 80% of European managers measured thought leadership by counting website and social hits ([Funds Society](https://www.fundssociety.com/en/news/negocio/effective-communication-is-european-asset-managers-greatest-challenge/)). The second is the cheap-click leakage to consumer audiences described earlier. The third is misreading webinar benchmarks: ON24's averages of 185 attendees and 46 minutes for financial-services webinars ([Business Wire](https://secure.businesswire.com/news/home/20250428478975/en/Financial-Services-Firms-Deepen-Client-Engagement-with-ON24-powered-Digital-Experiences)) are a ceiling for a boutique, not a target.

The operating model can stay lean. It needs one marketing-operations and data lead who owns the account universe, audiences and holdouts, and one or two content leads with protected time from the portfolio managers. A compliance partner works inside the process from a library of pre-approved claims and modular templates. AI pre-review tools such as Saifr were built for US FINRA and SEC rules ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220124005136/en/Fidelity-Investments-Launches-Innovative-New-Regulatory-Technology-RegTech-Business-to-Help-Financial-Institutions-Create-Compliant-Public-Communications)) and would need configuring for FCA and ESMA standards before they speed anything up.

## Conclusion

For this audience, most "advertising" turns out to be infrastructure. Four things do the persuading: the fund's data record across vendors and AI agents, a verified professional identity layer, a modular library of approved content, and a due-diligence process that answers in days. Paid media's narrower job is to make named people remember two or three buying situations before their review window opens. That shifts where a boutique can win. It cannot outspend large managers on reach. It can, however, beat them on access, because its portfolio managers are reachable, and on speed: a DDQ answered in five days, a market-triggered note live in 24 hours. It can also beat them on specificity, with one cluster, one language and one entry point at a time. The sequencing follows. First fix the data, crawler access and professional gates, which is cheap and compounds. Then run LinkedIn clusters and search under a holdout design. Then add owned assets, CPD, office hours and an index, one at a time. Enterprise ABM suites, European CTV and ChatGPT ads can wait.

Two developments make this more urgent than it looks. The AI research layer turns machine-readable fund data into a distribution channel whose compliance has to live in the data itself, because an AI client can drop the warnings that a factsheet carries. The one UK example the team inspected first-hand, a public MCP server returning performance with no warnings, shows how easily that goes wrong. The research found no UK or European manager in the major AI connector directories, so a well-governed early entry could become a lasting habit inside selectors' tools. And because the research found no UK or European manager that has published holdout-based proof that digital marketing moves professional flows, the first boutique to do so would gain the internal case for its budget and a distinctive story to tell gatekeepers. The open questions remain real. There is no evidence yet on how professional selectors use generative AI to choose funds. Digital out-of-home, X, Meta, Reddit and messaging apps were not researched, and nearly all cost data is vendor-sourced. Each should be tested on a small scale before it is scaled up.
